## 1. General-class frequency privileges (§97.301, tested by G1A01–G1A11)

- **FACT:** §97.301 frequency bands apply to "an amateur station located within 50 km of the Earth's surface, within the specified ITU Region, and outside any area where the amateur service is regulated by any authority other than the FCC." — Source: §97.301 preamble (verbatim).
- **FACT:** A station with a Technician, General, Advanced, or Amateur Extra control operator may use all amateur bands at 50 MHz and above (Region 2 values: 6 m 50–54 MHz, 2 m 144–148 MHz, 1.25 m 222–225 MHz, 70 cm 420–450 MHz, and all higher allocations). — Source: §97.301(a) table.
- **FACT:** General class HF/MF/LF privileges are exactly (all Regions unless noted): 2200 m 135.7–137.8 kHz; 630 m 472–479 kHz; 160 m 1800–2000 kHz (1810–1850 kHz in Region 1); 80 m 3.525–3.600 MHz; 75 m 3.800–4.000 MHz in Region 2 and 3.800–3.900 MHz in Region 3 (no Region 1 allocation); 60 m 5.3515–5.3665 MHz; 40 m 7.025–7.125 MHz and 7.175–7.300 MHz (7.175–7.200 MHz in Regions 1/3); 30 m 10.100–10.150 MHz; 20 m 14.025–14.150 MHz and 14.225–14.350 MHz; 17 m 18.068–18.168 MHz; 15 m 21.025–21.200 MHz and 21.275–21.450 MHz; 12 m 24.890–24.990 MHz; 10 m 28.000–29.700 MHz. — Source: §97.301(d) table (verbatim edges, current text).
- **FACT:** Amateur Extra holds all General segments plus Extra-only HF segments: 3.500–3.525 MHz, 3.600–3.800 MHz, 7.000–7.025 MHz, 7.125–7.175 MHz, 14.000–14.025 MHz, 14.150–14.225 MHz, 21.000–21.025 MHz, and 21.200–21.275 MHz — i.e., the Extra-exclusive spectrum sits on 80, 40, 20, and 15 meters only. — Source: §97.301(b) vs §97.301(d) tables (basis of G1A08 answer B).
- **FACT:** On 80, 75, 40, 20, and 15 meters the General allocation excludes part of the band, so "portions where General class licensees cannot transmit" exist on 80, 40, 20, and 15 meters (and 75/80 count as the 80 m answer grouping) — on 60, 30, 17, 12, and 10 meters General has the entire band. — Source: §97.301(d) vs §97.301(b) (basis of G1A01 answer C: "80 meters, 40 meters, 20 meters, and 15 meters").
- **FACT:** General licensees are excluded from 7.125–7.175 MHz; they are not excluded from 28.000–28.025 MHz (CW permitted, entire 10 m band) or 21.275–21.300 MHz (inside the General 15 m phone segment). — Source: §97.301(d) + §97.305(a) (basis of G1A05 answer A).
- **FACT:** 21.300 MHz (21300 kHz) is inside the General class 15 m segment 21.275–21.450 MHz. — Source: §97.301(d) (G1A09 answer C).
- **FACT:** Where General does not get a band's entire voice segment, the General share is the UPPER frequency portion (e.g., 20 m phone 14.150–14.350 → General 14.225–14.350; 40 m 7.125–7.300 → General 7.175–7.300; 75 m 3.600–4.000 → General 3.800–4.000; 15 m 21.200–21.450 → General 21.275–21.450). — Source: §97.301(d) vs §97.305(c) phone-segment edges (G1A11 answer B).
- **FACT:** When the FCC designates the amateur service as secondary on a band, amateur stations "must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations in a primary service." — Source: §97.303 preamble (verbatim; G1A06 answer C).
- **FACT:** The United States, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands are in ITU Region 2; other U.S. insular areas are in Region 2 or 3. — Source: Note to §97.303 (paraphrase; G1E06 answer C).

## 2. Authorized emissions / modes (§97.305, §97.307, §97.309)

- **FACT:** "Except as specified elsewhere in this part, an amateur station may transmit a CW emission on any frequency authorized to the control operator." — Source: §97.305(a) (verbatim; G1A07 answer D — CW on the entire 10 m band).
- **FACT:** On 30 m (10.100–10.150 MHz) only RTTY and data emissions are authorized — no phone and no image anywhere in the band. — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(viii) (table row: entire band, RTTY/data only); G1A02 answer B, G1A03 answer B.
- **FACT:** 60 m emission authorization (CURRENT): phone, RTTY, and data on 5.332, 5.348, 5.3515–5.3665, 5.373, and 5.405 MHz, standard §97.307(f)(14); CW is additionally permitted under §97.305(a). — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(iii) (verbatim frequencies). See W1.
- **FACT:** [POOL-ERA] Before Jan 14, 2026, the 60 m row authorized phone, RTTY, and data on "5.332, 5.348, 5.3585, 5.373 and 5.405 MHz" — five discrete channels, no contiguous segment. — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(iii) as of 2023-07-01. See W1.
- **FACT:** On 10 m, RTTY/data occupy 28.0–28.3 MHz and phone/image occupy 28.3–29.7 MHz. — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(xvii)–(xx).
- **FACT:** On 60 m "A station may transmit only phone, RTTY, data, and CW emissions … Emissions must not exceed a bandwidth of 2.8 kilohertz." — Source: §97.307(f)(14)(i) (current, verbatim; G1C03 answer A "2.8 kHz" remains correct). See W1.
- **FACT:** A station transmitting RTTY/data using a specified digital code "may use any technique whose technical characteristics have been documented publicly, such as CLOVER, G-TOR, or PacTOR, for the purpose of facilitating communications." — Source: §97.309(a)(4) (verbatim; G1C07 answer C).
- **FACT:** RTTY/data emissions using an unspecified digital code "must not be transmitted for the purpose of obscuring the meaning of any communication." — Source: §97.309(b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** [POOL-ERA note] The HF symbol-rate limits (300 bauds below 28 MHz, §97.307(f)(3); 1200 bauds on 10 m, (f)(4)) were replaced by a 2.8 kHz authorized-bandwidth standard effective Dec 2023 (88 FR 85127); (f)(4) is now Reserved. No active General question tests maximum symbol rate (verified by full-text search of pool-general.json). — Source: §97.307(f)(3) current vs 2023-07-01. See W2.

## 3. Transmitter power standards (§97.313, tested by G1C01–G1C11, G1E08, G1B10)

- **FACT:** "An amateur station must use the minimum transmitter power necessary to carry out the desired communications." — Source: §97.313(a) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP." — Source: §97.313(b) (verbatim; basis of G1C02 12 m = 1500 W, G1C05 28 MHz General = 1500 W, G1C06 1.8 MHz = 1500 W).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit with a transmitter power output exceeding 200 W PEP … (1) On the 10.10-10.15 MHz segment." — Source: §97.313(c)(1) (verbatim; G1C01 answer A — 200 W PEP on 10.140 MHz).
- **FACT:** The 200 W PEP HF limits in §97.313(c)(2) (3.525–3.60, 7.025–7.125, 21.025–21.20, 28.0–28.5 MHz) apply only "when the control operator is a Novice Class operator or a Technician Class operator" — a General control operator may use up to 1.5 kW PEP on those segments. — Source: §97.313(c)(2) (verbatim qualifier).
- **FACT:** (CURRENT 60 m power) "No station may transmit on the frequencies 5.332, 5.348, 5.373, and 5.405 MHz in the 60 m band with a radiated power exceeding 100 W ERP. No station may transmit in the 5.3515-5.3665 MHz band with a radiated power exceeding 9.15 W ERP." — Source: §97.313(i) (verbatim; amended by 91 FR 1430, Jan 14, 2026). See W1.
- **FACT:** [POOL-ERA] Before Jan 14, 2026, §97.313(i) read: "No station may transmit with an effective radiated power (ERP) exceeding 100 W PEP on the 60 m band." — Source: §97.313(i) as of 2023-07-01. See W1.
- **FACT:** For 60 m ERP computation, "the transmitter PEP will be multiplied by the antenna gain relative to a half-wave dipole antenna. A half-wave dipole antenna will be presumed to have a gain of 1 (0 dBd). Licensees using other antennas must maintain in their station records either the antenna manufacturer's data on the antenna gain or calculations of the antenna gain." — Source: §97.313(i) (verbatim; unchanged by the 2026 amendment — G1C04 answer A remains correct).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit with a transmitter output exceeding 10 W PEP when the station is transmitting a SS emission type." — Source: §97.313(j) (verbatim; G1E08 answer B). Paragraph letter (j) is the same in pool-era and current text.
- **FACT:** 2200 m limit: 1.5 kW PEP transmitter power or 1 W EIRP radiated; 630 m limit: 500 W PEP or 5 W EIRP (1 W EIRP within 800 km of the Russian Federation in Alaska). — Source: §97.313(k), (l) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** PEP means "The average power supplied to the antenna transmission line by a transmitter during one RF cycle at the crest of the modulation envelope taken under normal operating conditions." — Source: §97.3(b)(9) (verbatim; with §97.313(b) this pins G1C11 answer D — the regulated measurement is PEP output from the transmitter).

## 4. Control operator and control types (§97.7, §97.9, §97.105, §97.3)

- **FACT:** "When transmitting, each amateur station must have a control operator," who must hold an FCC amateur operator/primary station grant on the ULS or be authorized for alien reciprocal operation. — Source: §97.7(a) (verbatim first clause; G1D05 answer A — remote control of a US station from abroad still requires a US operator/primary license).
- **FACT:** "The control operator must ensure the immediate proper operation of the station, regardless of the type of control," and "A station may only be operated in the manner and to the extent permitted by the privileges authorized for the class of operator license held by the control operator." — Source: §97.105(a), (b) (verbatim; basis of G1E02 — a 10 m repeater above 29.5 MHz needs a General-or-higher control operator because Technicians have no privileges above 28.5 MHz, §97.301(e)).
- **FACT:** Control operator = "An amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for the transmissions from that station to assure compliance with the FCC Rules." — Source: §97.3(a)(13) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Control point = "The location at which the control operator function is performed." — Source: §97.3(a)(14) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Local control = use of a control operator "who directly manipulates the operating adjustments in the station"; remote control = one "who indirectly manipulates the operating adjustments in the station through a control link"; automatic control = "devices and procedures for control of a station when it is transmitting so that compliance with the FCC Rules is achieved without the control operator being present at a control point." — Source: §97.3(a)(31), (39), (6) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** CSCE = "Certificate of successful completion of an examination." — Source: §97.3(a)(15) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** A telecommand station on or within 50 km of Earth's surface must have a radio or wireline control link (radio control links must use an auxiliary station), must limit transmissions to no more than 3 minutes after a control-link malfunction, and must be protected against unauthorized transmissions. — Source: §97.213(a)–(c) (paraphrase).

## 5. Auxiliary, repeater, beacon, space, automatically controlled digital (§§97.201–97.221)

- **FACT:** An auxiliary station may transmit only on the 2 m and shorter wavelength bands, except 144.0–144.5 MHz, 145.8–146.0 MHz, 219–220 MHz, 222.00–222.15 MHz, 431–433 MHz, and 435–438 MHz; it may be automatically controlled and may transmit one-way communications. — Source: §97.201(b), (d), (e) (paraphrase of the segment list).
- **FACT:** A repeater "may receive and retransmit only on the 10 m and shorter wavelength frequency bands except the 28.0-29.5 MHz, 50.0-51.0 MHz, 144.0-144.5 MHz, 145.5-146.0 MHz, 222.00-222.15 MHz, 431.0-433.0 Mhz, and 435.0-438.0 Mhz segments" — so on 10 m, repeater operation is confined to the portion above 29.5 MHz. — Source: §97.205(b) (verbatim; G1A10 answer D).
- **FACT:** A repeater may be automatically controlled, and "The control operator of a repeater that retransmits inadvertently communications that violate the rules in this part is not accountable for the violative communications." — Source: §97.205(d), (g) (verbatim (g)).
- **FACT:** Where two repeaters interfere, the licensees are equally responsible for resolving it unless one is frequency-coordinated — then the non-coordinated repeater's licensee bears primary responsibility. — Source: §97.205(c) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** "A beacon must not concurrently transmit on more than 1 channel in the same amateur service frequency band, from the same station location." — Source: §97.203(b) (verbatim; G1B02 answer A).
- **FACT:** "The transmitter power of a beacon must not exceed 100 W." — Source: §97.203(c) (verbatim; G1B10 answer C).
- **FACT:** A beacon may be automatically controlled on 28.20–28.30 MHz, 50.06–50.08 MHz, 144.275–144.300 MHz, 222.05–222.06 MHz, 432.300–432.400 MHz, or the 33 cm and shorter bands — the HF automatic-beacon segment is 28.20–28.30 MHz. — Source: §97.203(d) (verbatim list; G1B09 answer D).
- **FACT:** "A beacon may transmit one-way communications." — Source: §97.203(g) (verbatim). Beacon definition: "An amateur station transmitting communications for the purposes of observation of propagation and reception or other related experimental activities." — Source: §97.3(a)(9) (verbatim; G1B03 answer A).
- **FACT:** "Any amateur station may be a space station," any license class may be its control operator within class privileges, and a space station "must be capable of effecting a cessation of transmissions by telecommand whenever such cessation is ordered by the FCC." — Source: §97.207(a), (b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Space stations are authorized the 17 m, 15 m, 12 m, 10 m, 6 mm, 4 mm, 2 mm, and 1 mm bands plus the 7.0–7.1 MHz, 14.00–14.25 MHz, 144–146 MHz, 435–438 MHz, 2400–2450 MHz, 5.83–5.85 GHz, 10.45–10.50 GHz, and 24.00–24.05 GHz segments; a space station may automatically retransmit signals of Earth and other space stations and may transmit one-way communications. — Source: §97.207(c), (d), (e) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** An automatically controlled station may transmit RTTY or data "on the 6 m or shorter wavelength bands, and on the 28.120-28.189 MHz, 24.925-24.930 MHz, 21.090-21.100 MHz, 18.105-18.110 MHz, 14.0950-14.0995 MHz, 14.1005-14.112 MHz, 10.140-10.150 MHz, 7.100-7.105 MHz, or 3.585-3.600 MHz segments." — Source: §97.221(b) (verbatim; G1E11 answer D).
- **FACT:** Outside those segments, automatic control of RTTY/data is allowed on other authorized frequencies only if the station "is responding to interrogation by a station under local or remote control" and no transmission exceeds 500 Hz bandwidth — never on the §97.303(h) 60 m channels. — Source: §97.221(c)(1), (2) (verbatim (1); G1E03 answer A).
- **FACT:** §97.221 "does not apply to an auxiliary station, a beacon station, a repeater station, an earth station, a space station, or a space telecommand station." — Source: §97.221(a) (verbatim).

## 6. Operating rules: ID, one-way, prohibited, third-party, international (§§97.101–97.121)

- **FACT:** "In all respects not specifically covered by FCC Rules each amateur station must be operated in accordance with good engineering and good amateur practice" — the FCC is the arbiter of that standard. — Source: §97.101(a) (verbatim rule text; G1B11 answer A).
- **FACT:** "Each station licensee and each control operator must cooperate in selecting transmitting channels and in making the most effective use of the amateur service frequencies. No frequency will be assigned for the exclusive use of any station." — Source: §97.101(b) (verbatim; G2B01 answer C).
- **FACT:** "At all times and on all frequencies, each control operator must give priority to stations providing emergency communications, except to stations transmitting communications for training drills and tests in RACES." — Source: §97.101(c) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** The five frequencies G1E10 names (14.100, 18.110, 21.150, 24.930, 28.200 MHz) are NOT set aside by Part 97; avoiding them is good amateur practice because the NCDXF/IARU international beacon network operates there. The only automatic-beacon segment Part 97 itself designates is 28.20–28.30 MHz. — Source: §97.101(a) + §97.203(d) (G1E10 answer A). See W4.
- **FACT:** "Each amateur station, except a space station or telecommand station, must transmit its assigned call sign on its transmitting channel at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication"; unidentified transmissions are prohibited. — Source: §97.119(a) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** ID may be sent by CW (automatic device ≤ 20 wpm), by phone in English (phonetics encouraged), by a specified RTTY/data code when the communication uses RTTY/data, or by image conforming to §73.682(a) standards. — Source: §97.119(b)(1)–(4) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** A control operator exercising upgrade privileges under §97.9(b) must append an indicator: KT (Novice→Technician), AG (Novice/Technician→General), AE (→Amateur Extra). — Source: §97.119(f)(1)–(3) (verbatim codes; G1D06 — "AG" while operating with General privileges until the upgrade shows in the FCC database).
- **FACT:** At the end of international third-party communications, the station must also transmit the call sign of the station with which the third-party message was exchanged. — Source: §97.115(d) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** Permitted one-way transmissions include "Brief transmissions necessary to make adjustments to the station," brief transmissions establishing two-way contact, telecommand, emergency communications, "Transmissions necessary to assisting persons learning, or improving proficiency in, the international Morse code," information bulletins, and telemetry. — Source: §97.111(b)(1)–(7) (verbatim for Morse practice; G1B05 answer B).
- **FACT:** Two-way communication with amateur stations in other countries is permitted "except those in any country whose administration has notified the ITU that it objects to such communications." — Source: §97.111(a)(1) (verbatim; G1B08 answer B).
- **FACT:** Amateur stations may only exchange messages with other amateur stations (plus the specific exceptions of §97.111(a)(2)–(5), e.g., emergencies, RACES, Armed Forces Day tests) — there is no authorization to communicate with non-licensed Wi-Fi stations on 2.4 GHz or anywhere else. — Source: §97.111(a) (G1E07 answer D).
- **FACT:** "An amateur station shall not engage in any form of broadcasting" (broadcasting = "Transmissions intended for reception by the general public, either direct or relayed"), nor transmit one-way communications except as specifically provided. — Source: §97.113(b) + §97.3(a)(10) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Prohibited: communications for hire or material compensation; communications in which the licensee/control operator has a pecuniary interest (with narrow exceptions); music via phone except as provided; communications intended to facilitate a criminal act; "messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning"; obscene or indecent language; false or deceptive signals or identification. — Source: §97.113(a)(2)–(4) (verbatim for encoding clause; G1B07 answer B — abbreviations/Q-signals are fine if they do not obscure meaning).
- **FACT:** "No station shall retransmit programs or signals emanating from any type of radio station other than an amateur station, except propagation and weather forecast information intended for use by the general public and originated from United States Government stations, and communications, including incidental music, originating on United States Government frequencies between a manned spacecraft and its associated Earth stations" — and such retransmission "may not be conducted on a regular basis, but only occasionally." — Source: §97.113(c) (verbatim; G1B04 answer C).
- **FACT:** "No amateur station, except an auxiliary, repeater, or space station, may automatically retransmit the radio signals of other amateur station." — Source: §97.113(d) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Third-party traffic to any station within US jurisdiction is always permitted; to foreign jurisdictions only for emergency/disaster-relief communications or where that administration "has made arrangements with the United States to allow amateur stations to be used for transmitting international communications on behalf of third parties." — Source: §97.115(a)(1), (2) (verbatim for the agreement clause; G1E05 with §97.117).
- **FACT:** "Transmissions to a different country, where permitted, shall be limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character." — Source: §97.117 (verbatim; G1E05 answer C).
- **FACT:** A third party may participate in stating the message only while the control operator is present at the control point continuously supervising, and the third party must not be a former licensee whose license was revoked, suspended and still in effect, surrendered for cancellation after enforcement notice, or subject of an active amateur-service cease-and-desist order. — Source: §97.115(b)(1), (2) (paraphrase; G1E01 answer A).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit third party communications while being automatically controlled except a station transmitting a RTTY or data emission" — nothing in §97.115 restricts third-party traffic by local vs remote control, so third-party messages via remote control are permitted whenever third-party messages are permitted. — Source: §97.115(c) (verbatim) + §97.115 generally (G1E12 answer A).
- **FACT:** Third party communications = "A message from the control operator (first party) of an amateur station to another amateur station control operator (second party) on behalf of another person (third party)." — Source: §97.3(a)(47) (verbatim).

## 7. Antenna structures and RF exposure (§97.15, §97.13; tested by G1B01, G1B06, G0A03–G0A08)

- **FACT:** "Owners of certain antenna structures more than 60.96 meters (200 feet) above ground level at the site or located near or at a public use airport must notify the Federal Aviation Administration and register with the Commission as required by part 17 of this chapter." — Source: §97.15(a) (verbatim; G1B01 answer C — 200 feet).
- **FACT:** "State and local regulation of a station antenna structure must not preclude amateur service communications. Rather, it must reasonably accommodate such communications and must constitute the minimum practicable regulation to accomplish the state or local authority's legitimate purpose. See PRB-1, 101 FCC 2d 952 (1985)." — Source: §97.15(b) (verbatim; G1B06 answer D).
- **FACT:** "Antennas used to transmit in the 2200 m and 630 m bands must not exceed 60 meters in height above ground level." — Source: §97.15(c) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** "A station within 1600 m (1 mile) of an FCC monitoring facility must protect that facility from harmful interference." — Source: §97.13(b) (verbatim; part of G1E04 answer D).
- **FACT:** Before transmitting from anywhere the station could cause human exposure above §1.1310 limits, the licensee must ensure compliance with §§1.1307(b), 2.1091, and 2.1093; evaluation guidance is in OET Bulletin 65 (Supplement B for amateur service); household members may be evaluated against occupational/controlled limits, others against general-population/uncontrolled limits. — Source: §97.13(c)(1) (paraphrase; G0A03 answer D, G0A06 answer A).
- **FACT:** "If the routine environmental evaluation indicates that the RF electromagnetic fields could exceed the limits contained in §1.1310 of this chapter in accessible areas, the licensee must take action to prevent human exposure to such RF electromagnetic fields." — Source: §97.13(c)(2) (verbatim; G0A05 answer A, G0A08 answer C).

## 8. RACES (§97.407; tested by G2B09, G2B11)

- **FACT:** RACES = "A radio service using amateur stations for civil defense communications during periods of local, regional or national civil emergencies." — Source: §97.3(a)(38) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** "No person may be the control operator of an amateur station transmitting in RACES unless that person holds a FCC-issued amateur operator license and is certified by a civil defense organization as enrolled in that organization." — Source: §97.407(a) (verbatim; G2B09 answer A).
- **FACT:** RACES stations share the control operator's amateur bands/emissions, except under the President's War Emergency Powers (47 U.S.C. 606) when only part 214 segments may be used. — Source: §97.407(b) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** "Communications for RACES training drills and tests … may not exceed a total time of 1 hour per week," except up to 72 hours no more than twice per calendar year with the approval of the chief officer for emergency planning of the applicable state/territory. — Source: §97.407(d)(4) (verbatim for the 1-hour limit; G2B11 answer C).

## 9. Exams and the VE system as G1 tests it (§§97.501–97.523, §97.9, §97.21, §97.505)

- **FACT:** General class requires passing (or credit for) Elements 2 and 3; Element 3 is 35 questions on General privileges, minimum passing score 26. — Source: §97.501(b), §97.503(b) (verbatim numbers).
- **FACT:** "Each examination for an amateur operator license must be administered by a team of at least 3 VEs at an examination session coordinated by a VEC." — Source: §97.509(a) (verbatim; G1D04 answer A with (b)(3)(i) — for a Technician exam, at least three VEs of General class or higher).
- **FACT:** Each administering VE must be accredited by the coordinating VEC, be at least 18 years old, hold Amateur Extra/Advanced/General to administer Technician exams, Amateur Extra/Advanced for General exams, Amateur Extra for Extra exams, and must never have had an amateur license revoked or suspended. — Source: §97.509(b)(1)–(4) (paraphrase; G1D02 answer C, G1D07 answer C, G1D08 answer B, G1D10 answer B). No US-citizenship requirement appears — a non-US citizen holding an FCC General-or-higher license can be an accredited VE.
- **FACT:** Written question sets must be prepared by an Amateur Extra VE, except Element 3 may be prepared by an Advanced VE and Element 2 by an Advanced or General VE; questions must come from the applicable published pool. — Source: §97.507(a), (b) (paraphrase).
- **FACT:** "All VECs must cooperate in maintaining one question pool for each written examination element. Each question pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions required for a single examination" and must be published before use. — Source: §97.523 (verbatim).
- **FACT:** Element credit: expired-and-beyond-grace General/Advanced (or pre-March-21-1987 Technician) grants get Element 3 credit; expired Extra gets Elements 3 and 4; Technician Plus or post-1987 Technician expired beyond grace gets no credit. Unexpired (or in-grace) General/Advanced/pre-1987 Technician gets Elements 2 and 3. — Source: §97.505(a) table (paraphrase; G1D01 answer A, G1D11 answer D — an expired General beyond grace shows proof of the grant and passes current Element 2).
- **FACT:** "A person whose amateur station license grant has expired may apply to the FCC for renewal of the license grant for another term during a 2 year filing grace period … Unless and until the license grant is renewed, no privileges in this part are conferred." — Source: §97.21(b) (verbatim).
- **FACT:** A Novice/Technician/General/Advanced licensee who has passed the higher-class exam, submitted FCC Form 605 to the administering VEs, and holds a CSCE "is authorized to exercise the rights and privileges of the higher operator class until final disposition of the application or until 365 days following the passing of the examination, whichever comes first." — Source: §97.9(b) (verbatim; G1D03 answer C, G1D06 with §97.119(f)(2), G1D09 answer C — CSCE valid 365 days; §97.505(b) confirms VEs must credit each element a CSCE shows passed within the previous 365 days).
- **FACT:** A VEC must have a written agreement with the FCC, exist to further the amateur service, coordinate exams for all license classes, and register every qualified examinee without regard to race, sex, religion, national origin, or organization membership. — Source: §97.521 (paraphrase; paragraph (b), the VEC-region requirement, was Reserved by 90 FR 57712, Dec 12, 2025 — see W3).

## 10. Watch items (pool-vs-current-rule tensions)

- **W1 — 60 m is the hazard area (91 FR 1430, eff. Jan 14, 2026).** The pool was written (2022) against the pre-2026 rule: five discrete channels only — 5332, 5348, 5358.5, 5373, 5405 kHz center (carriers 5330.5, 5346.5, 5357.0, 5371.5, 5403.5 kHz) — USB-only phone/data/RTTY plus CW, ≤2.8 kHz, and a flat 100 W ERP cap (POOL-ERA §§97.301(d) "See §97.303(h)", 97.303(h)(1), 97.305(c)(3)(iii), 97.307(f)(14), 97.313(i)). The CURRENT rule is materially different: amateurs may transmit anywhere in the contiguous 5351.5–5366.5 kHz segment (at only **9.15 W ERP**) PLUS on four of the five old channels — 5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz center (at **100 W ERP**); the old 5358.5 kHz channel is gone as a discrete channel (it lies inside the new segment); CW carrier sits at the center frequency while phone/data/RTTY carriers may be set 1.5 kHz below center on the discrete channels; the ≤2.8 kHz bandwidth cap and the non-dipole antenna-gain record requirement survive verbatim (§§97.303(h)(3), 97.305(c)(3)(iii), 97.307(f)(14), 97.313(i)). Impact on the active pool: G1C03 (answer 2.8 kHz) and G1C04 (antenna-gain records) remain literally correct; G1A01's correct set (80/40/20/15 m) is unchanged because General keeps full 60 m access under both texts. But any book prose describing 60 m as "five channels, USB only, 100 W ERP" is now WRONG — describe current text and flag the pool-era framing. §97.301(d)'s 60 m row itself changed from "See §97.303(h)" to "5.3515-5.3665" for all Regions.
- **W2 — HF digital symbol-rate rules changed (88 FR 85127, eff. Dec 2023, after the pool was written).** §97.307(f)(3) now imposes a 2.8 kHz authorized-bandwidth standard below 28 MHz (300-baud limit retained only for 2200 m/630 m) and (f)(4) is Reserved. No active General question tests maximum symbol rate (pool full-text search: zero "maximum symbol rate"/baud-limit questions; only G2E14/G8B10/G8C04 mention baud topically). Safe to teach current text; do not resurrect 300-baud as an exam fact.
- **W3 — Other post-pool Part 97 amendments, no G1 answer impact.** 90 FR 57712 (Dec 12, 2025) Reserved §97.521(b) (VEC regions), Reserved §97.315(b)(2) (pre-1978 amplifier waiver clause), and removed §§97.27 and 97.29; §97.207(g) space-station notification/debris rules were also expanded (88 FR 21451, 89 FR 65223). A full 2023-07-01 vs 2026-07-20 diff confirms §§97.505, 97.509, 97.221, 97.115, 97.119, 97.407, 97.13, 97.15 are byte-identical to pool-era text.
- **W4 — Facts resting on practice, not Part 97 text.** G1E10's beacon frequencies (14.100/18.110/21.150/24.930/28.200 MHz) are the NCDXF/IARU network, not a Part 97 set-aside; the exam's own ref is §97.101. G1B02's distractors (National Beacon Organization, internet posting) have no rule basis — only §97.203(b) is real. G1E06 additionally cites the ITU Radio Regulations (outside Part 97; Region 2 pinned via the Note to §97.303).
- **W5 — G1D12 citation oddity.** The published pool cites [97.507] for G1D12 (remote-controlling a South American station from the US; answer: only that country's regulations apply). §97.507 is "Preparing an examination" — an apparent misprint in the pool document. The substantive basis is territorial: Part 97 authorizes operation only where the FCC regulates the amateur service (§97.301 preamble: bands available "outside any area where the amateur service is regulated by any authority other than the FCC"). The answer is still correct as published.
