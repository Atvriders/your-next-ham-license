## 1. The upgrade exam (Element 3)

- **FACT:** To upgrade from Technician to General you pass examination Element 3 only — "General Class operator: Elements 2 and 3"; Element 2 is already credited from the Technician license you hold, so no re-testing of Element 2. — Source: 47 CFR §97.501(b) with §97.505(a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.501 (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** "Each applicant must pass an examination for a new amateur operator license grant and for each change in operator class." — Source: 47 CFR §97.501, eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** "Element 3: 35 questions concerning the privileges of a General Class operator license. The minimum passing score is 26 questions answered correctly." — Source: 47 CFR §97.503(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.503 (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The 2023–2027 Element 3 exam is built as 35 questions drawn one per group from the pool's 35 groups, out of a 423-question pool (10 subelements G1–G9 and G0). — Source: `canon/pool-general.json` (counts re-verified by parse on 2026-07-24: 423 questions, 35 groups, 10 subelements); `canon/ingestion-report.md` §5.1
- **FACT:** Every examination question set must utilize questions taken from the applicable published question pool, and each pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions on a single exam and be published and available to the public before use. — Source: 47 CFR §97.507(b) and §97.523 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)

## 2. Element credit and the CSCE (§97.505)

- **FACT:** Administering VEs must give Element 2 credit to an examinee holding an unexpired (or in-grace-period) Technician license granted on or after March 21, 1987; a Technician license expired beyond the grace period earns no credit. — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(a)(3), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.505 (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** An unexpired (or in-grace-period) Advanced, General, or pre-March 21, 1987 Technician license earns Elements 2 and 3 credit (Element 3 only if expired beyond grace); an expired-beyond-grace Amateur Extra license earns Elements 3 and 4. — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(a)(1)–(2), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** "The administering VEs must give credit to an examinee holding a CSCE for each element the CSCE indicates the examinee passed within the previous 365 days." — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Each CSCE conveying element credit is individually valid for 365 days from its issue date, and no subsequently issued CSCE renews another CSCE's validity period. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, CSCE section, http://www.arrl.org/files/file/VEs/VE%20Manual%20Web%20Final%202022.pdf (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)

## 3. The upgrade-immediacy rule — YES, upgrades differ from new licenses

- **FACT:** "The person named in an operator license grant of Novice, Technician, General or Advanced Class, who has properly submitted to the administering VEs a FCC Form 605 document requesting examination for an operator license grant of a higher class, and who holds a CSCE indicating that the person has completed the necessary examinations within the previous 365 days, is authorized to exercise the rights and privileges of the higher operator class until final disposition of the application or until 365 days following the passing of the examination, whichever comes first." — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.9 (verbatim, verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Contrast with a brand-new licensee: a first-time candidate has no operating authority until the license grant appears in the FCC's ULS database ("Your operating authority begins when your license grant information appears on the amateur service licensee database of the Universal Licensing System"). The wait-for-the-grant rule applies to new licenses only; an existing licensee upgrading operates immediately under §97.9(b). — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The exact mechanism has two conditions, both satisfied at the exam session: (1) the Form 605 requesting the higher class has been properly submitted to the administering VEs, and (2) the examinee holds the CSCE showing the required element(s) passed. From that moment — before the VEC files anything and before ULS changes — the new privileges are legal to use. — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** While operating under §97.9(b) the station must include an indicator after the call sign: "For a control operator who has requested a license modification from Novice or Technician to General Class: AG." — Source: 47 CFR §97.119(f)(2), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.119 (verbatim, verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The indicator is appended to the call sign, "separated from the call sign by the slant mark (/) or by any suitable word that denotes the slant mark." — Source: 47 CFR §97.119(c), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** In practice (one VEC's instructions): on phone, say your call sign followed by "temporary AG" (e.g., "This is KX9ABC temporary AG"); on CW or digital modes, sign KX9ABC/AG; once the upgrade is shown in the FCC ULS database, drop the suffix. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "I just upgraded. How long do I have to wait before I can use my new license privileges?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php (extracted from raw HTML 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The §97.9(b) interim authority ends at "final disposition of the application" — normally the grant appearing in ULS — or 365 days after passing, whichever comes first; after the grant shows, the authority rests on the license itself and §97.9(a). — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(a)–(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)

## 4. What changes when the grant lands

- **FACT:** You keep your call sign when you upgrade: "The station is reassigned its same call sign upon renewal or modification of its license, unless the licensee applies for a change to a new sequentially assigned or vanity call sign on FCC Form 605." — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Once the ULS record shows General class, the full new privileges are usable without the /AG indicator; the person named in the operator license grant "is authorized to be the control operator of an amateur station with the privileges authorized to the operator class specified on the license grant." — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(a), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24); Laurel VEC FAQ (2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** An amateur service license "is normally granted for a 10-year term." — Source: 47 CFR §97.25 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** If a candidate passes multiple exam elements at one session, the VEC transmits one application to the FCC reflecting the highest license class earned. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-24)

## 5. Exam session logistics (process facts, unchanged for upgraders)

- **FACT:** "Each examination for an amateur operator license must be administered by a team of at least 3 VEs at an examination session coordinated by a VEC." — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(a), eCFR versioner API, issue date 2026-07-22 (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** To administer a General class exam, each VE must be accredited by the coordinating VEC, be at least 18 years old, hold an Amateur Extra or Advanced class license (a General-class VE may not administer Element 3), and never have had an amateur license revoked or suspended. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(b)(3)(ii), eCFR versioner API (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** VEs must grade each examination element immediately upon completion (for remotely administered exams, at the earliest practical opportunity), and the VEs alone determine correctness. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(h) (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** When an examinee passes, 3 VEs certify that the examinee is qualified for the license grant, and the VEs must issue a Certificate of Successful Completion of Examination (CSCE); when an examinee fails, the VEs must return the application document and inform the examinee of the grade. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(i), (l) and (j) (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** No compromised examination may be administered, and the same question set may never be re-administered to the same examinee. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(f), eCFR versioner API (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** ARRL VEC retest policy: a failed element may be retaken at the same session only if the team has a different version of that element the applicant has not taken, the team has the time, resources, and willingness, and the applicant pays an additional test fee; nothing in FCC rules entitles a failed candidate to an immediate retest. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, "Retesting"; Laurel VEC FAQ (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** After a successful exam the VEs submit the application to the coordinating VEC, which screens the information, resolves discrepancies, and forwards all required data to the FCC electronically. — Source: FCC, Volunteer Examiner Coordinators, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/volunteer-examiner-coordinators (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** VECs and VEs may only be reimbursed for out-of-pocket expenses incurred in preparing, processing, administering, or coordinating an examination — the legal basis for exam session fees. — Source: 47 CFR §97.527 (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** A typical ARRL VEC session tests about ten people and lasts about 3.5 hours. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** Candidates must present one legal photo ID (or two forms of non-photo ID if none), and every applicant must answer the Basic Qualification Question (felony conviction status). — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** The NCVEC Quick-Form 605 is the standard exam-session application: the applicant completes Section 1 (email address and FRN are mandatory), the three administering VEs print, sign, and date Section 2, and the form goes to the coordinating VEC — never directly to the FCC. — Source: ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions, http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** The current NCVEC Form 605 is the 2022 edition (HTTP 200, application/pdf). — Source: NCVEC, https://www.ncvec.org/downloads/NCVEC_Form_605_2022.pdf (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** An FRN (10-digit FCC Registration Number, obtained by registering in CORES and available immediately) is required before exam day; the SSN is given to the FCC inside CORES, not on the exam form; a valid email address is mandatory on the application because the FCC sends all correspondence, including the official license copy, by email. — Source: FCC CORES FAQ, https://apps.fcc.gov/cores/html/know.html; ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session (lifted, verified 2026-07-23; safe wording per Tech canon §7.2 — never print "free of charge")
- **FACT:** Exams are offered both in person and as remote video-supervised online sessions; remote availability depends entirely on the individual VE team (Laurel VEC, for example, runs in-person exams only). — Source: ARRL, Find an Amateur Radio License Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session (HTTP 200 re-verified 2026-07-24); Laurel VEC FAQ (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)
- **FACT:** HamStudy.org's session page lists both in-person and remote exam sessions run by many VE teams. — Source: https://hamstudy.org/sessions (HTTP 200 re-verified 2026-07-24)

## 6. Fees in 2026 — and the upgrade exemption

- **FACT:** The 2026 ARRL VEC exam session fee is $15.00, and that one fee pays for one attempt at each of the three exam elements. — Source: ARRL VEC Exam Fees ("THE 2026 ARRL VEC EXAM FEE IS $15.00"), http://www.arrl.org/arrl-vec-exam-fees (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Candidates younger than 18 pay a reduced $5 ARRL VEC exam session fee ("The Youth Exam Fee For 2026 is $5.00"), regardless of exam level. — Source: ARRL VEC Exam Fees, http://www.arrl.org/arrl-vec-exam-fees (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The FCC's $35 application fee (effective April 19, 2022) still applies per application to new-license, renewal, rule-waiver, and vanity-call-sign applications. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Upgrading is exempt from the $35 FCC fee: "modification applications to upgrade an amateur radio licensee's operator class ... are exempt from fees" — so an upgrader has no FCC payment step (the 10-day payment window applies only to fee-bearing applications such as new licenses). — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** VECs and VE teams must not collect the $35 FCC fee at exam sessions; when a fee is due it is paid online directly to the FCC through CORES, never to the VE team. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Laurel VEC charges no fees for any licensing-related services. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, https://larc-vec.org/faq.php (lifted, verified 2026-07-23)

## 7. Pool currency (2023–2027 General pool)

- **FACT:** The 2023–2027 General Class (Element 3) pool is effective for exams from 2023-07-01 and is valid until 2027-06-30. — Source: ARRL, Question Pools ("GENERAL Class (Element 3) Pool is effective July 1, 2023 and is valid until June 30, 2027"), http://www.arrl.org/question-pools (re-verified 2026-07-24); NCVEC release page, https://ncvec.org/index.php/2023-2027-general-question-pool-release
- **FACT:** The NCVEC Question Pool Committee released the 2023–2027 General (Element 3) pool into the public domain ("The NCVEC Question Pool Committee hereby releases into public domain the 2023-2027 General, Element 3, Question pool"). — Source: NCVEC release page (text captured in `canon/source/release-page.html`, fetched 2026-07-24 per `canon/ingestion-report.md` §1)
- **FACT:** Question pools are reviewed and updated on a four-year rotation (Technician 2026–2030; General 2023–2027; Extra 2024–2028; "No question pools will be updated or released in 2025 or 2029") — so the next General pool takes effect 2027-07-01. — Source: ARRL, Question Pools, http://www.arrl.org/question-pools (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The 2023–2027 General pool carries a six-errata history: Errata 1 (2023-02-01, 9 modified + G9C06/G9D13 withdrawn), Errata 2 (2023-04-10, 3 modified + G6B09 withdrawn), 3rd errata (2023-12-01, FCC rule change; G2E12 modified + G1C08/G1C10 withdrawn), 4th errata (2024-03-06, G1E09 withdrawn), 5th errata (2024-11-08, G8C01 withdrawn), 6th errata (2026-02-04, G1A04/G1C09 withdrawn) — 9 withdrawals total; the 6th-errata release is the current document and there is no 7th errata as of 2026-07-24. — Source: `canon/ingestion-report.md` §5.2 (errata sheets cross-checked against the NCVEC release page)
- **FACT:** The active pool is 423 questions (432 published IDs minus 9 withdrawn, never renumbered, leaving exactly the gaps G1A04, G1C08, G1C09, G1C10, G1E09, G6B09, G8C01, G9C06 and the truncated end of G9D), in 35 groups across 10 subelements; one graphic (Figure G7-1) is referenced by 5 questions (G7A09–G7A13). — Source: `canon/ingestion-report.md` §5.1, §5.3, §5.4; counts re-verified by parse of `canon/pool-general.json` on 2026-07-24

## 8. What General opens (the honest privilege summary)

- **FACT:** "The General class license grants some operating privileges on all Amateur Radio bands and all operating modes. This license opens the door to world-wide communications." — Source: ARRL, Getting Licensed, http://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Per the rules, a General-class control operator is authorized transmitting segments on every MF/HF band — 2200 m, 160 m, 630 m, and the HF bands 80 m through 10 m (e.g., ITU Region 2: 3.525–3.600 MHz and 3.800–4.000 MHz on 80/75 m; 7.025–7.125 and 7.175–7.300 MHz on 40 m; 14.025–14.150 and 14.225–14.350 MHz on 20 m; all of 17 m, 12 m, and 10 m) — plus all amateur bands above 30 MHz shared with Technician, Advanced, and Amateur Extra. — Source: 47 CFR §97.301(d) and §97.301(a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.301 (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Which emission types go in which segments (phone/image vs. RTTY/data) is set by §97.305 — e.g., 20 m: RTTY/data 14.00–14.15 MHz, phone/image 14.15–14.35 MHz. — Source: 47 CFR §97.305, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.305 (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** Honest framing: General does not convey everything — the lowest slices of several HF bands remain Advanced/Amateur Extra territory (compare §97.301(b)–(c) with §97.301(d); e.g., Extra gets 3.500–3.600 and 3.600–4.000 MHz on 80/75 m, General only 3.525–3.600 and 3.800–4.000 MHz). — Source: 47 CFR §97.301(b)–(d), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** ARRL publishes graphical frequency-allocation charts by license class — the canonical visual reference for the band-by-band detail. — Source: ARRL, Graphical Frequency Allocations, https://www.arrl.org/graphical-frequency-allocations (HTTP 200, verified 2026-07-24)

## 9. What's next after General

- **FACT:** Amateur Extra requires passing Element 4 (with credit for Elements 2 and 3 already earned): "Amateur Extra Class operator: Elements 2, 3, and 4." — Source: 47 CFR §97.501(a), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** "Element 4: 50 questions concerning the privileges of an Amateur Extra Class operator license. The minimum passing score is 37 questions answered correctly." — Source: 47 CFR §97.503(c), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** The current Extra class (Element 4) pool is effective 2024-07-01 and valid until 2028-06-30. — Source: ARRL, Question Pools, http://www.arrl.org/question-pools (re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** "The Amateur Extra class license conveys all available U.S. Amateur Radio operating privileges on all bands and all modes." — Source: ARRL, Getting Licensed, http://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed (verbatim, re-verified 2026-07-24)
- **FACT:** A CSCE from passing Element 3 also counts toward Extra for 365 days (it evidences Element 3 credit), so an examinee may attempt Element 4 at the same session or within the CSCE window without re-passing Element 3. — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(b), eCFR (verified 2026-07-24)
