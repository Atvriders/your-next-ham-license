### Beat 1 — Feed lines: characteristic impedance and loss (G9A01–G9A03, G9A05, G9A06, G9A11)
- A parallel-conductor feed line's characteristic impedance is set by the **conductor spacing (center-to-center) and the conductor radius** — not by length, not by frequency (G9A01). Window/ladder line is approximately **450 ohms** (G9A03). Amateur coax is usually 50 ohms.
- Coax loss **rises with frequency** (G9A05) and is quoted in **dB per 100 feet** (G9A06 — the pool's unit). Two subtle effects: high SWR makes a *lossy* line lose **more** (G9A02); line loss makes SWR measured *at the shack end* look **better than it really is** (G9A11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-feedline-basics}}`.
- Vocabulary: characteristic impedance, coaxial cable, ladder line/window line, velocity factor, loss (dB/100 ft), feed line.

### Beat 2 — Standing waves, SWR, and matching (G9A04, G9A07–G9A10)
- Reflected power comes from a **mismatch between the feed line and the antenna feed-point impedance** (G9A04); the way to eliminate standing waves is to match the feed point to the line (G9A07).
- SWR from a resistive mismatch: Z_load ÷ Z₀ or Z₀ ÷ Z_load, whichever is ≥ 1 — 200 Ω on 50 Ω coax is **4:1** (G9A09); 10 Ω on 50 Ω coax is **5:1** (G9A10). **SWR is always stated with the larger number first** (never "1:4").
- The perennial trap (G9A08, cross-ref ch04's G4A06): a tuner at the transmitter does **not** change the SWR on the feed line — the line still runs 5:1; the tuner only presents 1:1 to the radio.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-standing-waves}}`.
- **Smith-chart sidebar (canon §7.6, binding):** one brief "what a Smith chart shows" sidebar — a graphical map of impedance and reflection used for matching design. **No exam weight, no dedicated figure** (zero pool questions test it); the glossary entry stays consistent.
- Vocabulary: SWR, standing wave, reflected power, forward power, feed-point impedance, impedance matching, Smith chart (sidebar only).

### Beat 3 — Dipoles, verticals, and the 468 formula (G9B)
- Patterns: a free-space half-wave dipole radiates a **figure-eight pattern broadside to the wire** with nulls off the ends (G9B04); a quarter-wave ground plane is **omnidirectional in azimuth** (G9B03).
- Height and feed point: below a half-wavelength high, a horizontal dipole's high-angle pattern goes **nearly omnidirectional** (G9B05) and its feed-point impedance **steadily decreases** toward 0.1-wavelength height (G9B07); moving the feed point from the center toward the ends **raises** the feed-point impedance (G9B08); ground-plane radials **sloped downward** raise the roughly 35 Ω feed point toward 50 Ω (G9B02); radials for a ground-mounted vertical lie **on the surface or buried a few inches** (G9B06). Horizontal polarization's HF advantage is **lower ground losses** (G9B09). A random wire connected directly to the rig can put **significant RF current on station equipment** (G9B01).
- The length formulas (the chapter's signature math, in §5): half-wave dipole L(ft) = 468/f(MHz) — 33 ft at 14.250 MHz (G9B10), 132 ft at 3.550 MHz (G9B11); quarter-wave monopole L(ft) = 234/f(MHz) — 8 ft at 28.5 MHz (G9B12). The 468 constant includes the ~5 percent end-effect shortening; answers are approximate.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-antenna-heights}}`.
- Vocabulary: dipole, ground plane, radials, figure-eight pattern, omnidirectional, polarization, random wire, resonant frequency.

### Beat 4 — Yagis, gain, and matching devices (G9C)
- Yagi anatomy: driven element ≈ a half wavelength (G9C02); the **reflector is longer** and the **director shorter** than the driven element (G9C03 — "the reflector is the big one behind"); a longer boom with more directors gives more gain (G9C05).
- Pattern vocabulary: the **main lobe** is the direction of maximum radiated field (G9C08); **front-to-back ratio** compares main-lobe power to the opposite direction (G9C07). Fatter elements widen bandwidth (G9C01); gain, front-to-back ratio, and SWR bandwidth all trade off through boom length, element count, and spacing (G9C10).
- Gain units: **dBi = dBd + 2.15** — dBi is the bigger number (G9C04). Two identical Yagis stacked a half-wavelength apart gain approximately **3 dB** over one (G9C09 — tie to "+3 dB doubles power").
- Matching devices: a **beta (hairpin) match** is a shorted stub at the feed point (G9C11); a **gamma match** needs no insulation of the driven element from the boom (G9C12).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch09-yagi-stack}}`.
- Vocabulary: Yagi, driven element, reflector, director, boom, main lobe, front-to-back ratio, dBi, dBd, stacking, beta match, gamma match.

### Beat 5 — The specialized zoo (G9D)
- NVIS antennas: **horizontal dipoles 0.1 to 0.25 wavelengths up** for short-skip regional coverage (G9D01 — low, the opposite instinct from DX; the propagation mode is ch03's). An **end-fed half-wave** presents a very high feed-point impedance (G9D02). A **halo** is omnidirectional in its own plane (G9D03).
- Multiband strategies: **traps** make one antenna multiband (G9D04) — and multiband antennas pay with **poor harmonic rejection** (G9D11). Stacking antennas vertically narrows the main lobe in **elevation** (G9D05). A **log-periodic** trades everything for wide bandwidth, element length and spacing varying logarithmically along the boom (G9D06, G9D07).
- Mobile and receiving exotica: a **screwdriver antenna** tunes by varying its base-loading inductance (G9D08); a **Beverage** is a directional **receiving** antenna for MF and low HF (G9D09); an electrically small loop's **nulls are broadside to the loop** (G9D10 — the mirror of the dipole's broadside maxima; students swap these, so draw both); a dipole with a single central support is an **inverted V** (G9D12).
- Vocabulary: NVIS antenna, end-fed half-wave, halo, trap antenna, log-periodic, screwdriver antenna, Beverage antenna, small loop, inverted V, loading.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote; the length formulas and SWR are the chapter's signature math)

- Dipole length (G9B10/G9B11): "A half-wave dipole for 14.250 MHz: L = 468 ÷ 14.250 = 32.8 — approximately **33 feet**. For 3.550 MHz: 468 ÷ 3.550 = 131.8 — approximately **132 feet**. (The 468 constant already includes the ~5 percent end-effect shortening, so the answer is approximate by design.)"
- Quarter-wave vertical (G9B12): "A quarter-wave monopole for 28.5 MHz: 234 ÷ 28.5 = 8.2 — approximately **8 feet**."
- SWR from a resistive mismatch (G9A09/G9A10): "A 200 Ω load on 50 Ω coax: 200 ÷ 50 = **4:1**. A 10 Ω load on the same coax: 50 ÷ 10 = **5:1**. Always divide the bigger number by the smaller so the ratio comes out ≥ 1 — and state it larger-number-first."
- Gain units (G9C04): "A Yagi advertised at 5 dBd is the same antenna as 5 + 2.15 = 7.15 dBi — dBi is always the bigger number."
