### Beat 1 — RF exposure principles (G0A01, G0A02, G0A04, G0A07, G0A12)
- RF energy's established effect on body tissue is **heating** — RF is non-ionizing radiation, so "radiation poisoning" is not the hazard (G0A01; the distractors borrow ionizing-radiation language).
- Exposure depends on **frequency, power density, and duty cycle — all three** (G0A02): frequency because the body absorbs some bands more efficiently (MPE limits vary with frequency, lowest at 50 MHz among the pool's bands); duty cycle because exposure averages over time.
- The applicability threshold: all stations with a time-averaged transmission of more than **one milliwatt** are subject to the FCC's RF-exposure rules (G0A12 — the most-misremembered number in the subelement).
- **Time averaging** = total exposure averaged over a period (G0A04), which is why a lower duty cycle permits higher power for the same exposure (G0A07). Controlled vs uncontrolled environments: household members may be evaluated against occupational/controlled limits; the general public gets general-population/uncontrolled limits.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch10-duty-factor}}`.
- Vocabulary: non-ionizing radiation, MPE, power density, duty cycle, duty factor, time averaging, controlled/uncontrolled environment.

### Beat 2 — Evaluating your station (G0A03, G0A05, G0A06, G0A08–G0A11)
- The duty: a station that doesn't meet the exemption criteria must **perform an exposure evaluation** per FCC OET Bulletin 65 (G0A06). Acceptable ways to show compliance: **OET-65 calculation, computer modeling, or measurement with calibrated field-strength equipment — any of the three** (G0A03, a keyed "all these choices are correct").
- The ongoing duty: **evaluate the station routinely** and keep people out of identified high-exposure areas (G0A08); re-evaluate when you change power, antenna, or operating habits. Figure: `{{fig:ch10-mpe-evaluation}}`.
- If an evaluation shows limits could be exceeded: act to **prevent human exposure** — power down, raise the antenna, restrict access (G0A05, §97.13(c)(2)). If a directional antenna's main lobe could hit a neighbor's space, **ensure it cannot be pointed at them while they are present** (G0A10); indoor antennas must keep occupied areas under MPE limits (G0A11).
- Measuring RF fields for compliance requires a **calibrated field-strength meter with a calibrated antenna** — SWR meters and receivers don't qualify (G0A09).
- Vocabulary: OET Bulletin 65, exposure evaluation, field-strength meter, calibrated antenna, exemption criteria.

### Beat 3 — Wiring and the NEC (G0B01–G0B03, G0B05, G0B06)
- In a 240 VAC four-conductor circuit, **only the hot wires** get fuses or breakers — never the neutral or the ground (G0B01; if the neutral fuse blows first, the chassis can sit at line voltage).
- The wire/breaker pairs (an inverse pair of questions — learn both directions): a **20 A circuit needs AWG 12 minimum** (G0B02); **AWG 14 wire pairs with a 15 A fuse or breaker** (G0B03). NEC ampacity, NFPA 70.
- The **National Electrical Code (NEC)** covers the electrical safety of the station (G0B06). A **GFCI** trips when current flows from a hot wire directly to ground — the missing return current is what it detects (G0B05).
- Vocabulary: NEC (NFPA 70), AWG, hot/neutral/ground, circuit breaker, fuse, GFCI.

### Beat 4 — Grounding, lightning, and towers (G0B04, G0B07, G0B08, G0B11, G0B13)
- The lightning-protection ground system goes **outside the building** (G0B04); lightning arrestors mount **where feed lines enter the building** (G0B13); **all ground rods must be bonded together** with the other premises grounds (G0B11 — separate "independent" grounds are the hazard; cross-ref ch04's bonding).
- Tower work: confirm the harness is **rated for the climber's weight and within its service life** (G0B07); before climbing a tower with powered devices, **lock out and tag every circuit feeding the tower** (G0B08).
- Vocabulary: lightning arrester, ground rod, bonding, lockout/tagout, climbing harness.

### Beat 5 — Everyday shop hazards (G0B09, G0B10, G0B12)
- Generators run in **well-ventilated areas** — carbon monoxide is the killer (G0B09). Lead-tin solder's danger is **lead contaminating food via unwashed hands** (G0B10). A power-supply **interlock** removes dangerous voltages when the cabinet is opened (G0B12).
- Vocabulary: interlock, carbon monoxide, lead solder hygiene.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote)

- Duty-factor averaging (G0A04/G0A07 applied): "Two 100 W PEP stations, same band, same antenna. The SSB ragchewer's duty factor is roughly 40 % during transmissions and he talks half the time: average exposure contribution ≈ 100 × 0.4 × 0.5 = 20 W. The FT8 operator keys 100 % duty factor in every other 15-second slot: ≈ 100 × 1.0 × 0.5 = 50 W — two and a half times the exposure from the same '100-watt' rig. That's why a lower duty cycle permits higher power for the same exposure."
- The 1 mW threshold in practice (G0A12): "The exposure rules reach essentially every amateur station: the applicability floor is a time-averaged transmission of more than one milliwatt — a 100 W rig exceeds it even if you transmitted only a few seconds per hour. Plan to evaluate, not to claim an exemption."
