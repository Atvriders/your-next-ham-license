### Beat 1 — HF phone and the sideband conventions (G2A + G2B04/G2B05)
- The convention trio (canon §7.3, pin all three precisely): **voice** — LSB below 10 MHz (160/75/40 m), USB at 10 MHz and above (20, 17, 15, 12, 10 m, and VHF/UHF SSB), custom not law or physics (G2A01–G2A04, G2A09); **60 m phone is USB by rule** (canon §7.1); digital conventions come in Beat 5. **Anti-shorthand law: never teach "LSB below 14 MHz" — the boundary is 10 MHz and 30/17/12 m are USB.**
- HF voice is single sideband, period (G2A05): one sideband transmitted, carrier and other sideband suppressed (G2A07); buys less bandwidth and better power efficiency (G2A06) — SSB is the narrowest phone emission.
- The dial reads the suppressed-carrier frequency; speech energy spreads ~3 kHz to one side — above the dial on USB, below it on LSB. Minimum separations: 150–500 Hz CW, 2–3 kHz SSB (G2B04, G2B05). (Full band-edge arithmetic is ch04's; here the concept.)
- Breaking in: say your call sign once during a pause (G2A08). "CQ DX" from the lower 48 invites answers from outside the lower 48 (G2A11). VOX = hands-free switching; PTT is the safer default in a noisy shack (G2A10). ALC: set mic gain so ALC just begins to work on peaks — past that, flat-topping and splatter (G2A12; one knob linking G8A08/G8A10/G8C13).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch02-bandplan-map}}`.
- Vocabulary: USB/LSB, suppressed carrier, sideband convention, VOX, PTT, ALC, CQ DX, band plan.

### Beat 2 — Courtesy, emergencies, and band plans (G2B)
- Nobody owns a frequency (§97.101(b)): except during emergencies no amateur has priority — not nets, not QSOs, not contests (G2B01); stations that begin interfering work it out mutually (G2B03). Emergencies are the one priority exception (§97.101(c)).
- The pre-calling ritual: listen, then "Is this frequency in use?" + your call on phone, "QRL?" + your call on CW; follow the voluntary band plan (G2B06, G2B07).
- Distress: acknowledge the station first and determine what assistance is needed (G2B02).
- RACES vs ARES: RACES is the Part 97 civil-defense service — control operators must hold an FCC amateur license and be certified by a civil defense organization (G2B09); routine RACES drills are limited to 1 hour per week (G2B11). ARES is the ARRL volunteer body anyone can join.
- Net discipline on HF: backup frequency ready (G2B10); the custom centers of activity — MMSN 14.300 MHz USB daily, Hurricane Watch Net 14.325 USB by day / 7.268 LSB by night — **traditions under §97.101(b), never "assigned emergency frequencies"** (canon §7.11; even HWN asks permission if the frequency is busy).
- The 6 m DX window 50.100–50.125 MHz: band-plan custom — US-48 stations work only stations outside the 48 contiguous states (G2B08); voluntary, not FCC law.
- Vocabulary: voluntary band plan, DX window, net control station (NCS), RACES, ARES, center of activity.

### Beat 3 — CW on HF (G2C)
- The pool's five Q-signals verbatim: QRS? = send slower (G2C02); QRL? = is this frequency in use? (G2C04); QSL = I have received and understood (G2C09); QRN = troubled by static (G2C10); QRV = ready to receive (G2C11). (QRN = natural static vs QRM = man-made — the classic swap.)
- Prosigns: KN = listening only for specific station(s) (G2C03); AR = end of a formal message (G2C08).
- Technique: full break-in (QSK) = receive between code characters and elements (G2C01); answer a CQ no faster than it was sent (G2C05); zero beat = match your transmit frequency to the received signal (G2C06); RST with a "C" suffix = chirpy signal (G2C07).
- Culture: cut numbers (5NN for 599), 73 is already plural (never "73s"), W1AW daily code practice 5–35 WPM.
- Vocabulary: Q signal, prosign, QSK/full break-in, zero beat, RST, chirp, cut numbers.

### Beat 4 — DXing, contests, and logging (G2D)
- Volunteer Monitors: amateurs formally enlisted via ARRL under an FCC agreement to watch the bands so the service self-regulates (G2D01, G2D02; replaced the Official Observer program in 2019); they can localize a stuck-carrier station by comparing beam headings from several locations (G2D03). VM ≠ VE, VM ≠ frequency coordinator.
- Maps and paths: azimuthal projection centered on your location = true bearings and distances, the DXer's map (G2D04); long path = point the beam 180 degrees from the short-path heading (G2D06).
- Split operation: the DX transmits on one frequency, listens on another — "listening 5 to 10 up" means call 5–10 kHz above his transmit frequency, never on it; say your own call, not his. Figure: `{{fig:ch02-split-operation}}`.
- Everyday craft: the CQ pattern (G2D05); NATO phonetics (G2D07); logs are voluntary but help answer FCC inquiries (G2D08); exchange signal reports first so both stations adapt (G2D11). **Contest framing (canon §7.11):** in contests the report is a fixed formality — "59"/"599" sent regardless of conditions; in ragchews an honest report is normal. Teach both.
- Contests: the exchange is the minimum that scores (call sign, report, sponsor's fields — real ARRL examples from canon §2.5); contest rules never override FCC identification rules (G2D09); no contests on the WARC bands (30/17/12 m) by long truce. Figure: `{{fig:ch02-contest-exchange}}`.
- QRP = low-power operation; the ~5 W figure is club/award custom, never a regulation (G2D10, canon §7.11). QSLing: "QSL" = "I have received and understood" (cross G2C09); LoTW cross-matching; the bureau route (rate cited "as of 2026-07-24" or described without prices — canon §7.11).
- Vocabulary: Volunteer Monitor, azimuthal projection, short/long path, split operation, pileup, contest exchange, QRP, LoTW, QSL bureau.

### Beat 5 — Digital operating on HF (G2E)
- The opposite-conventions pair (canon §7.3 — the pool tests both): **AFSK RTTY → LSB** (G2E01, the traditional convention — state the practice only, no origin story); **JT65/JT9/FT4/FT8 → USB on every band**, including 80/40 m where voice is LSB (G2E05).
- RTTY: 170 Hz shift most common on HF (850 Hz was the old commercial standard) (G2E06); mark and space tones; properly tuned but won't decode → reversed mark/space, wrong baud rate, or wrong sideband (G2E14). (Baudot mechanics are ch08's.)
- FT8: computer time accurate to about 1 second (G2E07 — the exam-facing value; ARRL's "within 2 seconds" may appear only as operating color, canon §7.11); bad-clock symptom = waterfall full of signals, zero decodes; answer a CQ on a clear frequency in the **alternate** time slot, never on the caller's own offset in his slot (G2E04); watering holes — the pool keys approximately 14.074–14.077 MHz (G2E15); most 20 m digital activity sits in 14.070–14.100 MHz (G2E08); PSK31 hangs out at 14.070 MHz.
- Winlink and friends: Winlink = amateur radio email — wireless, VHF and HF, a form of packet radio, all three true (G2E12); a "Remote Message Server" is a gateway (G2E13); connect by transmitting a connect message on its published frequency (G2E10); VARA is a digital protocol used with Winlink (G2E02); PACTOR connections are strictly two-station ARQ links you cannot join in progress (G2E09); interference to a PACTOR/VARA link = retries, timeouts, pauses, failed connects (G2E03).
- AREDN: high-speed amateur mesh data networking for emergencies and community events (G2E11).
- Vocabulary: AFSK, mark/space, shift, baud, time slot, waterfall, Winlink, VARA, PACTOR, gateway, ARQ, AREDN.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote)

- Split arithmetic: "A rare DX station on 14.205 MHz announces 'listening 5 to 10 up.' You keep your receive VFO on 14.205 and set your transmit VFO anywhere from 14.210 to 14.215 — inside his listening window, never on his own frequency. Both frequencies must be clear before you call."
- Occupancy and separation: "Your USB dial reads 14.250 MHz, so your signal occupies roughly 14.250–14.253 MHz. An SSB neighbor needs 2–3 kHz of separation; a CW neighbor can live 150–500 Hz away — which is why the CW end of the band packs more conversations into less spectrum."
- FT8 timing (short): "FT8 runs alternating 15-second slots: a minimal six-message contact — CQ, reply, report, report+RR, RRR, 73 — completes in about 90 seconds when both stations answer in the alternate slot."
