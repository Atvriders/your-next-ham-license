### Beat 1 — The HF station, end to end (G4A's receiver/radio questions)
- The block tour: transceiver, power supply, computer/sound-card interface, antenna tuner, wattmeter/SWR meter, feed line, antenna — what each does and where it sits in the signal and control flows. Figure: `{{fig:ch04-hf-station}}`.
- Receiver toolbox, three noise fighters kept apart (the pool tests them separately): a **notch filter** removes an interfering carrier inside the passband (G4A01); a **noise blanker** mutes receiver gain during each noise pulse (G4A03); **DSP noise reduction** turned up too far distorts the desired signal (G4A07). Also: the receive **attenuator** prevents overload from strong signals (G4A13); "reverse" sideband on CW can move an interfering signal out of the passband (G4A02); dual VFOs enable split operation — transmit on one frequency, listen on another (G4A12, cross-ref ch02).
- The electronic keyer generates the dots and dashes (G4A10).
- Vocabulary: notch filter, noise blanker, DSP noise reduction, attenuator, split operation, electronic keyer.

### Beat 2 — Amplifiers, ALC, and the antenna tuner (rest of G4A)
- Vacuum-tube RF amplifier tune-up: adjust TUNE for a pronounced **dip** in plate current (resonance) (G4A04); set LOAD/COUPLING for the desired power without exceeding maximum plate current (G4A08).
- ALC between exciter and amplifier prevents excessive drive (G4A05) — but with AFSK data modes the ALC must be **inactive**, because its action distorts the signal (G4A11).
- Hot-switching: delay RF output after keying an external amplifier so its relays can switch the antenna first — hot-switching damages the relays (G4A09).
- The antenna tuner truth (a trap the pool sets twice — here and G9A08): it increases power transfer by making the **transmitter** see its design load; it does **not** change the SWR on the feed line to the antenna (G4A06).
- Vocabulary: plate current dip, ALC (automatic level control), hot-switching, antenna tuner (coupler/transmatch).

### Beat 3 — The test bench (G4B)
- Oscilloscope: contains horizontal and vertical channel amplifiers (G4B01); beats a DVM for viewing complex waveforms (G4B02); **the** instrument for checking a CW keying waveform (G4B03); displays an RF envelope when fed attenuated transmitter RF output into the vertical input (G4B04).
- Meters: voltmeters use high input impedance to avoid loading the circuit (G4B05); a DMM's advantage is higher precision (G4B06); an analog meter wins when adjusting for a peak or a null (G4B09).
- Two-tone test: feed two non-harmonically related audio tones into an SSB transmitter and examine the output — it analyzes **linearity** (G4B07, G4B08), not carrier suppression.
- Directional wattmeter: reads forward and reflected power, from which SWR follows (G4B10). Antenna analyzer: connects to the antenna and feed line (G4B11), can measure coax impedance (G4B13); nearby strong signals can corrupt its SWR readings (G4B12). An analyzer cannot measure front-to-back ratio or transmitter power.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch04-test-gear}}`.
- Vocabulary: oscilloscope, DMM, input impedance, two-tone test, linearity, directional wattmeter, forward/reflected power, antenna analyzer, dummy load.

### Beat 4 — RFI, grounding, and bonding (G4C)
- Cures: a bypass capacitor shunts RF to ground, curing RFI in audio circuits (G4C01); a ferrite choke on a cable kills common-mode RFI current (G4C08). Wideband interference across many frequencies indicates arcing at a poor electrical connection (G4C02).
- What RFI sounds like in a consumer audio device: SSB = distorted speech (G4C03); CW = on-and-off humming or clicking (G4C04). (Rectification mangles it — "clearly audible speech" is the trap.)
- RF burns and hot chassis come from a ground wire with high impedance at the operating frequency — e.g., a resonant ground connection (G4C05, G4C06). Never solder lightning-protection ground joints — lightning heat destroys solder (G4C07).
- Bonding: bond equipment enclosures together to minimize ground loops (symptom: hum on transmitted audio) (G4C09, G4C10) and to minimize RF hot spots in the shack (G4C11); ground all metal enclosures so hazardous voltages can never appear on the chassis (G4C12).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch04-grounding-bonding}}`.
- Vocabulary: RFI, bypass capacitor, arcing, common-mode current, ferrite choke, ground loop, bonding, chassis ground.

### Beat 5 — Speech processing, S meters, and the band edges (G4D)
- Speech processor: raises **average** power while the peaks stay legal, increasing apparent loudness (G4D01, G4D02); misadjusted it produces distorted speech, excess intermodulation, and excessive background noise — all three (G4D03). ("Increases peak power" is the trap.)
- S meter: measures received signal strength (G4D04); one S unit ≈ 6 dB (G4D06); 6 dB = 4× power, so S8→S9 takes 4× the transmit power (G4D07); "20 dB over S9" = 100× more powerful than S9 (G4D05). (The dB formula itself is ch05's; here the anchors, worked in §5.)
- SSB band-edge arithmetic (the chapter's calculation drill): the rig displays the suppressed-carrier frequency; USB occupies carrier → carrier+3 kHz, LSB occupies carrier−3 kHz → carrier. Worked: LSB displayed at 7.178 MHz occupies 7.175–7.178 MHz — **landing exactly on the General 40 m phone boundary from G1A05 (cross-reference ch01, canon §7.10)** (G4D08); USB displayed at 14.347 MHz occupies 14.347–14.350 MHz (G4D09). Rules of thumb: with 3 kHz LSB stay at least 3 kHz **above** a lower band edge (G4D10); with 3 kHz USB at least 3 kHz **below** an upper edge (G4D11).
- Vocabulary: speech processor, average vs peak power, S meter, S unit, suppressed carrier, occupied bandwidth.

### Beat 6 — Mobile and portable power (G4E)
- HF mobile antennas: a capacitance hat electrically lengthens a physically short whip (G4E01); a corona ball bleeds off RF voltage at the whip tip to prevent discharge (G4E02); shortened antennas have high Q and very limited operating bandwidth (G4E06); antenna efficiency is the biggest limit on an HF mobile station's performance (G4E05).
- Mobile DC wiring: a 100-watt HF rig connects **directly to the battery** with heavy-gauge fused wire — the cigarette-lighter/auxiliary socket wiring cannot carry the current (G4E03, G4E04). Vehicle receive noise comes from the charging system, the fuel delivery system, and control computers — all of them (G4E07).
- Solar: cells are wired in series-parallel for useful voltage and current (G4E08); one silicon photovoltaic cell gives about 0.5 V open-circuit in full sun (G4E09); a series diode stops night-time back-discharge (G4E10); lithium iron phosphate batteries require a charge controller with the panel (G4E11).
- Vocabulary: capacitance hat, corona ball, electrically short antenna, high Q, photovoltaic cell, series diode, charge controller, LiFePO4.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote; both fit)

- S-meter power math (G4D05–G4D07): "Your contact reads you S8 and asks for S9. One S unit ≈ 6 dB ≈ 4× power, so your 100 W becomes 400 W. And when the big station down the band is '20 dB over S9,' that's 10^(20/10) = 100× the power of S9 — dB math ch05 formalizes; the anchors do the exam work here."
- Band-edge occupancy (G4D08/G4D09): "Your LSB dial reads 7.178 MHz; the signal occupies 7.175–7.178 MHz — legal by a hair, because the General 40 m phone segment starts at 7.175 MHz (G1A05, chapter 1). On USB at 14.347 MHz the signal occupies 14.347–14.350 MHz, inside the 14.350 MHz upper edge. The 3 kHz rules of thumb follow directly: LSB dial ≥ 3 kHz above a lower edge; USB dial ≤ 3 kHz below an upper edge."
