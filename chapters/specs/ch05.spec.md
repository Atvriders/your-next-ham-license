### Beat 1 — Reactance: AC meets the coil and the capacitor (G5A's reactance questions)
- Reactance X: opposition to AC current flow caused by capacitance or inductance (G5A02, G5A03, G5A04); unit = ohm (G5A09); letter = X (G5A11). Resistance dissipates power; reactance stores and returns it — no heat.
- The two directions to memorize: an inductor's reactance **rises** with frequency (G5A05); a capacitor's **falls** (G5A06). Amplitude does not change reactance (distractors).
- The formulas, taught for real (the pool tests them conceptually; the book teaches them): $X_L = 2\pi f L$ and $X_C = 1/(2\pi f C)$ — each used at least once with pool numbers (see §5). Figure: `{{fig:ch05-reactance-curves}}` with one worked point each from the pool's own components.
- Phase (conceptual, honest): voltage leads current in an inductor, lags in a capacitor — the seed of impedance and resonance. Sidebar figure: `{{fig:ch05-time-constants}}` (RC charge/discharge and RL current curves; brief context for how C and L behave in switched DC circuits — no exam weight, one paragraph).
- Vocabulary: reactance, inductance, capacitance, phase, henry, farad.

### Beat 2 — Impedance and resonance (G5A's impedance/resonance questions)
- Impedance Z = the ratio of voltage to current — Ohm's law generalized to AC (G5A08); admittance = the reciprocal of impedance (G5A07). (Conductance/susceptance/reluctance are the distractor vocabulary — name them once.)
- Impedance magnitude and phase: $|Z| = \sqrt{R^2 + X^2}$, with the 50 Ω + j50 Ω → 70.7 Ω, φ = 45° walk-through. Figure: `{{fig:ch05-impedance-vectors}}`.
- Resonance: X_L = X_C and the two reactances **cancel** (G5A12); in a **series** LC circuit that makes impedance very **low**, in a **parallel** LC circuit very **high** (G5A01 — distractor A swaps them). The resonant-frequency formula $f = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ used once with pool numbers (see §5). Q as the sharpness of the resonance curve (concept). Figure: `{{fig:ch05-resonance-curves}}`.
- Impedance matching at RF can use a transformer, a pi-network, or a transmission-line section — all three (G5A10).
- Vocabulary: impedance, admittance, resonance, resonant circuit, Q, impedance matching.

### Beat 3 — Power, RMS, and PEP (G5B's power questions)
- The three DC power forms with one-step-harder numbers: P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R — 400 VDC across 800 Ω → 200 W (G5B03); 12 V × 0.2 A = 2.4 W (G5B04); 7.0 mA through 1,250 Ω ≈ 61 mW (G5B05); inverse: 50 Ω load dissipating 1,200 W has ≈ 245 V RMS across it (G5B12). Parallel branches: total current = sum of branch currents (G5B02).
- RMS: the AC value that heats a resistor exactly like the same-value DC (G5B07). For a sine wave V_rms = V_peak/√2 ≈ 0.707 × V_peak, and V_pp = 2√2 × V_rms ≈ 2.828 × V_rms: 17 V peak ≈ 12 V RMS (G5B09); 120 V RMS ≈ 339.4 V peak-to-peak (G5B08).
- PEP (peak envelope power): power at the crest of the modulation envelope — PEP = (V_pp/(2√2))²/R = V_pp²/(8R): 200 V peak-to-peak across 50 Ω = 100 W (G5B06); 500 V p-p = 625 W (G5B14). The distractor game: using V_pp directly in P = V²/R yields the plausible 400 W/2,500 W wrong answers — the √2 conversion is the whole game. For an unmodulated carrier PEP = average power, ratio 1.00 (G5B11): a 1,060 W average carrier is 1,060 W PEP (G5B13).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch05-rms-peak-pep}}`.
- Vocabulary: RMS, peak, peak-to-peak, PEP, average power, modulation envelope, dummy load.

### Beat 4 — Decibels for real (G5B01, G5B10 + the ch04 anchors)
- The defining formula is first-class exam math in this book (canon §3): $dB = 10 \log_{10}(P_2/P_1)$. Anchors: ×2 ≈ 3 dB (G5B01); 6 dB = ×4; 10 dB = ×10; 20 dB = ×100 (cross-ref ch04's S meter).
- G5B10 is the pool's only non-round dB computation — give the derivation, not just the answer: a 1 dB loss leaves 10^(−0.1) = 0.794 of the power, a 20.6% loss.
- Worked twice in §5; a `> **The math, if you want it:**` sidebar may carry the logarithm refresher.
- Vocabulary: decibel (dB), logarithm (as needed).

### Beat 5 — Transformers and combining components (G5C)
- Transformer action = mutual inductance (G5C01); voltage ratio = turns ratio, and running it backwards inverts the ratio (G5C02); a step-**up** transformer's primary carries the **higher** current and is wound with heavier wire (G5C05).
- Turns math: V_s = V_p × (N_s/N_p) — 120 VAC, 500-turn primary, 1,500-turn secondary → 360 V (G5C06). Impedance transforms as the **square** of the turns ratio: match 600 Ω to 50 Ω → 12:1 impedance ratio → turns ratio √12 ≈ 3.5:1 (G5C07). Figure: `{{fig:ch05-transformer}}`.
- Combination rules (R and L behave alike, C behaves opposite): series R/L add; parallel R/L add by reciprocals; series C adds by reciprocals; parallel C adds. The full pool drill set: 10‖20‖50 Ω ≈ 5.9 Ω (G5C03); 100‖200 Ω ≈ 67 Ω (G5C04); 20 mH + 50 mH = 70 mH (G5C11); three 10 mH parallel = 3.3 mH (G5C10); three 100 µF series = 33.3 µF (G5C09); 20 µF series 50 µF ≈ 14.3 µF (G5C12); 5 nF + 5 nF + 750 pF parallel = **10.750 nF** (G5C08 — the unit-mix drill; reproduce the keyed answer's trailing zero exactly). To increase capacitance add a capacitor in parallel; to increase inductance add an inductor in series (G5C13, G5C14).
- Vocabulary: mutual inductance, turns ratio, step-up/step-down transformer, impedance transformation, series/parallel equivalents.

## 5. Worked examples (several `> **Worked example:**` blockquotes — this is the calculation chapter; use the pool's own numbers exactly)

- Inductive reactance: "The pool's 20 mH inductor (G5C11) at 7 MHz: $X_L = 2\pi f L$ = 2π × 7×10⁶ × 0.020 ≈ 880,000 Ω. The same inductor at 60 Hz: ≈ 7.5 Ω. Frequency moves reactance — exactly G5A05's concept."
- Capacitive reactance + resonance: "The pool's 100 µF capacitor (G5C09) at 60 Hz: $X_C = 1/(2\pi f C)$ ≈ 26.5 Ω. Pair the pool's 10 mH (G5C10) with 100 µF: $f = 1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ ≈ 159 Hz — at 159 Hz, X_L = X_C ≈ 10 Ω and they cancel (G5A12)."
- Impedance magnitude: "50 Ω resistance in series with 50 Ω reactance: $|Z| = \sqrt{50^2 + 50^2}$ ≈ 70.7 Ω, phase angle 45°."
- PEP: "200 V peak-to-peak across a 50-ohm dummy load: PEP = V_pp²/(8R) = 200²/(8×50) = 100 W (G5B06). The trap: 200²/50 = 800 or (200/2)²/50 = 200-style missteps — convert to RMS first." (Also 500 V p-p → 625 W, G5B14.)
- Decibels: "A 1 dB cable loss: P₂/P₁ = 10^(−1/10) = 0.794, so 100 W in delivers 79.4 W — a 20.6% loss (G5B10). Doubling power: $dB = 10 \log_{10}(2)$ ≈ 3 dB (G5B01)."
- Transformer: "120 VAC into a 500-turn primary with a 1,500-turn secondary: 120 × (1500/500) = 360 V (G5C06). Matching 600 Ω to 50 Ω: impedance ratio 12:1, so turns ratio √12 ≈ 3.5:1 (G5C07)."
