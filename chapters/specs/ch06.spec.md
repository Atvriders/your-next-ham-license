### Beat 1 — Passives and power at General depth (G6A's passive/battery questions)
- Batteries: a standard 12-volt lead-acid battery should not be discharged below **10.5 V** for maximum life (G6A01); internal resistance acts like a series resistor — lower resistance means higher available discharge current (G6A02).
- Capacitor personalities: electrolytics pack high capacitance into a small volume but are leaky, loose-tolerance, polarized, and not for RF (G6A04); low-voltage ceramics are above all **cheap** (G6A08). The pool swaps these two answers — teach the pair.
- Parasitics: a wire-wound resistor is a coil — its **inductance** makes RF behavior unpredictable (G6A06); an inductor driven above its **self-resonant frequency** becomes **capacitive** (G6A11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch06-device-panel}}` (the whole chapter's component panel — introduce it here, refer back through Beat 3).
- Vocabulary: internal resistance, electrolytic capacitor, ceramic capacitor, self-resonant frequency, parasitic.

### Beat 2 — Diodes and transistors (G6A's semiconductor questions + G6B08)
- The threshold pair (the questions share one option set — each answer is the other's distractor; teach as a pair): germanium ≈ **0.3 V** forward threshold (G6A03), silicon junction ≈ **0.7 V** (G6A05). An LED emits light when **forward biased** (G6B08).
- A BJT used as a switch lives at its endpoints — **saturation** (fully on) and **cutoff** (fully off) (G6A07); the active region is where a *linear* transistor lives (the contrast that makes G6A07 make sense; amplifier classes are ch07's).
- A MOSFET's gate is insulated from the channel by a thin insulating layer (G6A09).
- Vacuum tubes (still alive in HF amplifiers): the **control grid** meters electron flow from cathode to plate (G6A10); the **screen grid** exists to reduce grid-to-plate capacitance (G6A12).
- Vocabulary: forward threshold voltage, anode, cathode, LED, saturation, cutoff, BJT, MOSFET, control grid, screen grid.

### Beat 3 — ICs, ferrites, and connectors (G6B)
- IC families: MMIC = Monolithic Microwave Integrated Circuit (G6B02); CMOS beats TTL on **power consumption** (G6B03); an op-amp is an **analog** IC (G6B06).
- Ferrites: the material **mix** sets the frequency range where a core works (G6B01); ferrite toroids give large inductance, frequency-optimized cores, and self-contained fields — all three (G6B05, one of the pool's keyed "all these choices are correct" answers); a ferrite bead on coax chokes **common-mode** shield current by putting impedance in that current's path (G6B10, cross-ref ch04's RFI cures).
- The connector table (pure FACT, drill as a table): BNC — bayonet, usable to about **4 GHz** (G6B04); type N — **moisture-resistant**, useful to **10 GHz** (G6B07); SMA — small threaded, good to several GHz (G6B11); **RCA phono** — the non-RF connector used for low-frequency or DC connections to a transceiver (G6B12; PL-259/BNC/N are all RF connectors — the question wants the odd one out).
- Vocabulary: MMIC, CMOS, TTL, op-amp, ferrite mix, toroid, common-mode current, BNC, type N connector, SMA, RCA phono.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote)

- Threshold math (G6A01 + G6A05 together): "Two silicon junction diodes in series between your 12 V battery and a project circuit drop about 0.7 V each: 12 − 0.7 − 0.7 = 10.6 V at the project — uncomfortably close to the 10.5 V floor where a lead-acid battery should stop being discharged. One diode (a reverse-polarity guard) leaves 11.3 V: cheap insurance, paid for in volts."
- Battery sag intuition (G6A02): "Internal resistance acts like a series resistor inside the battery. A pack with lower internal resistance shows less voltage sag under a 20 A transmit load — which is why 'higher available discharge current' is the answer, not more ampere-hours."
