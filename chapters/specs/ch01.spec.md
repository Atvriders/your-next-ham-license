### Beat 1 — Your new HF map (G1A)
- General class MF/HF/LF privileges exactly (canon §2.1 list, Region 2 focus): the band-by-band segments. The four bands with portions Generals cannot use: **80, 40, 20, 15 meters** (G1A01) — the lower edges are Extra/Advanced. Generals get the **entire** 160, 60, 30, 17, 12, and 10 m bands.
- Where General's share of a phone segment is partial, it is the **upper** frequency portion (G1A11): 20 m phone General 14.225–14.350; 40 m 7.175–7.300; 75 m 3.800–4.000; 15 m 21.275–21.450.
- Worked exclusions: Generals are excluded from 7.125–7.175 MHz on 40 m (G1A05) — but not from 28.000–28.025 (CW is allowed on the entire 10 m band, G1A07) or 21.275–21.300 (inside the General 15 m phone segment; 21.300 MHz is in, G1A09). Extra-only spectrum sits on 80/40/20/15 m only (G1A08).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch01-general-band-chart}}`.
- Vocabulary: allocation, primary service, secondary service, harmful interference, WARC bands.

### Beat 2 — Mode rules and sharing (rest of G1A)
- 30 m (10.100–10.150 MHz): RTTY and data only — no phone, no image anywhere in the band (G1A02, G1A03).
- CW may be transmitted on any frequency authorized to the control operator — that's why all of 10 m (28.0–29.7 MHz) is CW-legal (G1A07). On 10 m, RTTY/data occupy 28.0–28.3 and phone/image 28.3–29.7 MHz; repeaters live above 29.5 MHz (G1A10).
- Secondary allocation: "must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations in a primary service" (G1A06). The US, Puerto Rico, and the USVI are in ITU Region 2 (G1E06 — file it here with allocations).
- Vocabulary: emission mode, CW emission, phone/image, RTTY/data segments, ITU Region 2.

### Beat 3 — 60 meters as it exists NOW (G1C's 60 m questions)
- **Current-text law (canon §7.1, binding):** teach the two-part structure — (1) the contiguous 5351.5–5366.5 kHz segment at **9.15 W ERP**, and (2) four discrete channels 5332, 5348, 5373, 5405 kHz at **100 W ERP**; USB phone; **2.8 kHz maximum bandwidth** on all 60 m spectrum. Never describe 60 m as "five channels, USB only, 100 W ERP" — that rule is dead (the FCC's 91 FR 1430 amendment; the 6th errata withdrew the two conflicted questions).
- The surviving questions: G1C03 (2.8 kHz — cite §97.303(h)(3); the pool prints the superseded [97.303(h)(1)]) and G1C04 (antenna-gain records for non-dipole antennas — cite §97.313(i); the pool prints [97.303(i)]). Drill the keyed answers exactly as published; explain with the current sections.
- ERP arithmetic belongs here (see §5): ERP = transmitter PEP × antenna gain relative to a half-wave dipole; a dipole is presumed 0 dBd (gain 1).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch01-60m-structure}}`.
- Vocabulary: ERP (effective radiated power), EIRP, emission bandwidth, suppressed-carrier frequency.

### Beat 4 — Power rules (rest of G1C + G1E08)
- The default ceiling: 1.5 kW PEP output — the answer on 12 m, on 28 MHz, and on 1.8 MHz for a General (G1C02, G1C05, G1C06). The big HF exception: **200 W PEP on 30 m** (G1C01, asked as 10.140 MHz). The Novice/Technician 200 W segments (3.525–3.60, 7.025–7.125, 21.025–21.20, 28.0–28.5 MHz) do not bind a General control operator.
- PEP is the legal measurement: "the average power supplied to the antenna transmission line by a transmitter during one RF cycle at the crest of the modulation envelope" (G1C11) — full PEP math is ch05's; here pin the definition and the minimum-necessary-power rule (§97.313(a)).
- Spread spectrum: 10 W PEP maximum (G1E08). A new digital protocol must be publicly documented before use (G1C07); unspecified codes must never be used to obscure meaning (§97.309(b)).
- Vocabulary: PEP (peak envelope power), transmitter power output, spread spectrum, specified/unspecified digital code.

### Beat 5 — Antennas, beacons, and what you may not transmit (G1B + G1E04/G1E10)
- Antenna structures: the 200-foot (60.96 m) FAA-notification/FCC-registration threshold (G1B01); PRB-1 — local rules must reasonably accommodate amateur communications with the minimum practicable regulation (G1B06).
- Beacons: one channel per band per location, 100 W maximum, one-way transmissions for propagation observation (G1B02, G1B03, G1B10); automatically controlled beacons on HF only in 28.20–28.30 MHz (G1B09). The "National Beacon Organization" is fictional (canon §7.9).
- Keep two beacon facts apart (canon §7.9): the auto-beacon **segment** (28.20–28.30 MHz, G1B09) vs the **NCDXF/IARU beacon-network frequencies** 14.100/18.110/21.150/24.930/28.200 MHz — avoiding those is good amateur practice, not a Part 97 set-aside (G1E10). G1B09's 21.08–21.09 MHz distractor is a real segment — for automatically controlled *digital* stations, not beacons.
- One-way and prohibited transmissions: permitted one-way list incl. Morse-code practice transmissions (G1B05 — the pool prints the malformed citation `[97.111((5)(b)]`; cite §97.111(b) in prose, never "repair" the quote); occasional retransmission of US-government weather/propagation forecasts (G1B04); Q-signals and abbreviations are fine if they don't obscure meaning (G1B07); no broadcasting; any country except those that objected to the ITU (G1B08). Where the rules are silent: good engineering and good amateur practice, with the FCC as arbiter (G1B11).
- Interference-avoidance duties (G1E04): protect FCC monitoring facilities within 1600 m, secondary-allocation duties, spread-spectrum non-interference.
- Vocabulary: beacon, PRB-1, reasonable accommodation, one-way transmission, broadcasting, good amateur practice.

### Beat 6 — Examiners, credit, and the upgrade mechanics (G1D)
- The VE system as G1 tests it: at least 3 VEs per session; a General-class VE may administer **Technician** exams only (General exams need Amateur Extra or Advanced VEs); accredited by the VEC; 18+; no US-citizenship requirement; never revoked/suspended (G1D02, G1D04, G1D07, G1D08, G1D10).
- Element credit: expired General/Advanced (or pre-March-21-1987 Technician) grants earn credit per §97.505(a); beyond the 2-year grace period: show proof of the old grant and pass current Element 2 (G1D01, G1D11).
- The upgrade trio — drill hard: CSCE valid 365 days (G1D09); a Technician with a General CSCE may operate on any General/Technician segment immediately under §97.9(b) (G1D03), signing with the **AG** indicator whenever using General privileges until the upgrade shows in the FCC database (G1D06). Same wording law as ch00: both conditions + /AG in one breath (canon §7.2).
- Remote control across borders: run your US station from abroad and you still need your US operator/primary license (G1D05); run a South American station from the US and **only that country's regulations apply** (G1D12 — territorial jurisdiction; the pool prints the misprinted citation `[97.507]`, an apparent typo — cite the §97.301 preamble in prose; canon §7.4). Teach the pair together — the asymmetry surprises students.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch01-ag-indicator}}`.
- Vocabulary: VE, VEC, CSCE, element credit, grace period, control operator, control point, local/remote/automatic control.

### Beat 7 — Third-party traffic, automatic digital, and special stations (G1E)
- Third-party: revoked-former-licensees disqualified (G1E01); messages to agreement countries limited to amateur-service/personal/emergency content (G1E05 with §97.117); third-party via remote control is permitted whenever third-party traffic is permitted (G1E12); third-party participation needs the control operator present and supervising.
- Control categories: local / remote / automatic definitions (§97.3(a)); automatically controlled RTTY/data stations may operate on 6 m and shorter wavelengths plus the listed HF segments (G1E11); outside those segments, the initiating station must be under local or remote control and transmissions ≤ 500 Hz (G1E03).
- Special stations: auxiliary stations (2 m and shorter, with the excluded segments); repeaters may be automatically controlled and their control operators aren't accountable for inadvertent violative retransmission; the 10 m repeater rule — a Technician can talk *through* a 10 m repeater only if the repeater's own control operator holds General or higher (G1E02, canon §7.9).
- Amateurs may not communicate with non-licensed Wi-Fi stations on 2.4 GHz or anywhere else (G1E07).
- Vocabulary: third-party communications, third-party agreement, auxiliary station, repeater, automatic control, spread spectrum.
