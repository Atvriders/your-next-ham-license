### Beat 1 — Power supplies (G7A01–G7A08)
- The linear supply chain: transformer → rectifier → filter → regulator (Book 2 saw the boxes; now the internals). Figure: `{{fig:ch07-power-supply}}`.
- Rectifiers: **half-wave** = one diode, converts **180°** of each AC cycle (G7A04, G7A05); **full-wave** = converts **360°** (G7A06), so its unfiltered output is DC pulses at **twice the AC line frequency** (G7A07) — the fact that explains why full-wave is easier to filter. The center-tapped full-wave circuit uses **two diodes and a center-tapped transformer** (G7A03 — a full-wave **bridge** uses four; keep the two apart).
- Filters are built from capacitors and inductors (G7A02). The **bleeder resistor** discharges the filter capacitors when power is removed — a safety device (G7A01). A **switchmode** supply chops at high frequency, which is what allows smaller, lighter components (G7A08).
- Vocabulary: rectifier, half-wave rectifier, full-wave rectifier, center-tapped transformer, ripple frequency, bleeder resistor, switchmode power supply.

### Beat 3 — Amplifiers and oscillators (G7B's analog questions)
- Amplifier classes by conduction angle (figure: `{{fig:ch07-amp-classes}}`): **class A conducts 100 %** of the cycle — linear but inefficient (G7B04); **class C has the highest efficiency** (G7B02) but distorts amplitude, so it suits constant-envelope modes only — **FM yes; SSB and AM no** (G7B11). A **linear amplifier** preserves the input waveform — required for SSB (G7B10).
- **Neutralizing** an amplifier cancels internal feedback to eliminate self-oscillation (G7B01). Efficiency = RF output power ÷ DC input power (G7B08).
- Oscillators: a sine-wave oscillator = a filter plus an amplifier in a feedback loop (G7B07); an LC oscillator's frequency is set by the tank circuit's inductance and capacitance (G7B09).
- Vocabulary: class A/AB/B/C amplifier, linear amplifier, neutralization, efficiency, oscillator, tank circuit, feedback.

### Beat 4 — Digital building blocks (G7B03, G7B05, G7B06)
- An AND gate's output is high only when **both** inputs are high (G7B03); a 3-bit counter has 2³ = **8 states** (G7B05); a shift register is a clocked array passing data along in steps (G7B06).
- Vocabulary: AND gate, binary counter, shift register.

### Beat 5 — Transceivers, filters, and SDR (G7C)
- The SSB chain: a **balanced modulator** produces double-sideband suppressed-carrier RF (G7C02); a **filter** strips one sideband to make SSB (G7C01); a **product detector** recovers the audio in an SSB receiver (G7C04). (Impedance-matching roles — G7C03 — fold into the ch05/ch09 cross-references; one line here.)
- Filter vocabulary (figure: `{{fig:ch07-filter-responses}}`): **insertion loss** = attenuation *inside* the passband (G7C07); **cutoff frequency** = the half-power point of a low-pass filter (G7C12); **ultimate rejection** = the maximum stopband rejection (G7C13); band-pass bandwidth is measured between the upper and lower half-power frequencies (G7C14).
- Receivers and SDR: sensitivity depends on input gain, demodulator bandwidth, and noise figure — all three (G7C08, a keyed "all these choices are correct"); **DDS** gives variable frequency with crystal-oscillator stability (G7C05); **DSP** filters realize many bandwidths and shapes an analog filter cannot (G7C06); in an SDR the **I and Q signals are 90° apart** (G7C09), I/Q processing lets software create any modulation type (G7C10), and filtering, detection, and modulation all happen in software (G7C11).
- Vocabulary: balanced modulator, product detector, insertion loss, cutoff frequency, ultimate rejection, half-power point, sensitivity, noise figure, DDS, DSP, I and Q signals, SDR.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote)

- Ripple frequency (G7A07 applied): "A 60 Hz mains transformer feeding a full-wave rectifier: both halves of every cycle get flipped upright, so the raw DC pulses arrive at 2 × 60 = 120 Hz. The filter capacitor gets 120 charging chances per second — twice what a half-wave supply gives it — which is why full-wave supplies need less capacitance for the same ripple."
- Counter states (G7B05): "A 3-bit ripple counter: each bit doubles the state count — 2³ = 8 states (000 through 111) before it rolls over. A 4-bit counter would run 16."
- Efficiency concept (G7B08): "A final amplifier drawing 13.8 V × 10 A = 138 W from the supply while delivering 100 W of RF runs at η = 100 ÷ 138 ≈ 72 % efficiency — the other 38 W is heat the heat sink must move."
