### Beat 1 — The three modulations and the envelope (G8A01–G8A05, G8A07, G8A08, G8A10, G8A11)
- The definition table (teach as one table — the distractors are invented words like "phase convolution"): **AM** varies the signal's amplitude/instantaneous power (G8A05); **FM** varies its instantaneous frequency (G8A03); **PM** varies its phase angle (G8A02).
- **Direct FSK** means the digital signal drives the oscillator frequency itself (G8A01). **The subtle one:** a **reactance modulator** attached to an RF amplifier stage **after** the oscillator produces **phase modulation** (G8A04) — the reflex answer "FM" is wrong precisely because the modulator follows the oscillator; flag it with an aside.
- The AM specifics: the **modulation envelope** is the outline made by connecting the peaks of the RF waveform (G8A11); overdriving flattens the peaks — **flat-topping**, distortion from excessive drive or speech levels (G8A10); overmodulation splatters into **excessive bandwidth** (G8A08). SSB is the narrowest phone emission (G8A07).
- Vocabulary: AM, FM, PM, carrier, modulation envelope, flat-topping, overmodulation, reactance modulator, FSK.

### Beat 2 — Digital modulation and link arithmetic (G8A06, G8A09, G8A12–G8A14 + G8C15)
- **QPSK** sends data as 0°/90°/180°/270° phase shifts — two bits per symbol (G8A12); **QPSK31** is sideband-sensitive, has error correction, and fits about the same bandwidth as BPSK31 — all three (G8A06, a keyed "all these choices are correct").
- **FT8 is 8-tone FSK** (G8A09); an FT8 report of "+3" means an SNR of +3 dB in a 2.5 kHz bandwidth (G8C15) — not S-units, not dB over S9.
- Link budget: add transmit power and antenna gains and subtract all losses, as seen at the receiver (G8A13); link margin = the received level minus the minimum the receiver needs (G8A14).
- Vocabulary: PSK, QPSK, Varicode (pointer to Beat 5), link budget, link margin, SNR.

### Beat 3 — Mixing, superhets, and the deviation math (G8B01–G8B07, G8B11)
- Mixing two signals is **heterodyning** (G8B03); a mixer outputs the **sum and difference** of the local-oscillator and RF frequencies (G8B11); a superheterodyne receiver is tuned by varying its local oscillator (G8B01). The **image** is an unwanted response **twice the IF away** from the desired signal (G8B02 — twice the IF, not twice the signal frequency).
- A **multiplier** stage outputs a harmonic of its input — how a VHF FM transmitter reaches its operating frequency from a lower-frequency oscillator (G8B04).
- The chapter's two calculations (both in §5): **Carson's rule** for FM bandwidth, BW ≈ 2 × (peak deviation + highest modulating frequency) — 5 kHz deviation with 3 kHz audio gives 16 kHz (G8B06); **deviation through a multiplier chain** multiplies by the same factor as the carrier — 146.52 MHz ÷ 12.21 MHz = 12, so 5 kHz ÷ 12 = 416.7 Hz of oscillator deviation (G8B07).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch08-heterodyne}}`.
- Vocabulary: heterodyning, mixer, local oscillator, IF, image response, multiplier, deviation, Carson's rule.

### Beat 4 — Intermodulation, duty cycle, and bandwidth discipline (G8B05, G8B08–G8B10, G8B12, G8B13)
- Signals combining in a **non-linear** circuit spawn spurious products = **intermodulation** (G8B12); a product's order is the sum of its mixing coefficients; **odd-order** products land closest to the original frequencies (G8B05), and **2F1 − F2** is the odd-order example (order 3) (G8B13 — check the sums: 5F1−3F2 is order 8, 3F1−F2 is order 4, both even).
- High-duty-cycle modes (FT8, RTTY, FM) run the transmitter hard — average power can exceed ratings even when PEP is fine (G8B08; field practice: many run digital at reduced power — color, not exam).
- Matching receiver bandwidth to the mode gives the best signal-to-noise ratio (G8B09 — ~3 kHz SSB, ~500 Hz CW/RTTY, wide for the FT8 waterfall). Higher symbol rates require wider bandwidth (G8B10).
- **Symbol-rate rule change (canon §7.5, binding):** the FCC replaced the HF symbol-rate limits with a 2.8 kHz authorized-bandwidth standard below 28 MHz in December 2023; no active pool question tests maximum symbol rate. Teach the current 2.8 kHz standard and **never resurrect "300 baud" as an exam fact**.
- Sidebar figure: `{{fig:ch08-emission-designator}}` (anatomy of an emission designator, e.g. 2K80J3E — orientation only; no active question tests designator decoding, so one sidebar, no exam weight).
- Vocabulary: intermodulation, odd-order product, duty cycle, symbol rate, emission designator.

### Beat 5 — The digital modes in depth (G8C02–G8C16)
- RTTY: the 45.45-baud **Baudot** code — 5 bits plus start and stop bits (G8C04); its two tones are **mark and space** (G8C11); 170 Hz shift (cross-ref ch02's G2E06).
- PSK31: **Varicode** gives common letters short codes, so **uppercase letters take longer** (G8C12, G8C08); it hangs out at 14.070 MHz on 20 m (cross-ref ch02).
- WSPR: weak-signal propagation beacon mode — call sign, grid locator, and power in 2-minute sequences, reports mapped at WSPRnet (G8C02). FT8 decodes far below the noise (G8C07).
- Packet and error recovery: frames carry routing/handling information in the **header** (G8C03); **NAK** means "please retransmit" (G8C05); too many failed retries drop the connection (G8C06); **FEC** sends redundant information with the data so the receiver can fix errors without a repeat (G8C10). Mesh networks reroute around failed nodes (G8C09, AREDN cross-ref ch02).
- Waterfall literacy (figure: `{{fig:ch08-digital-waterfalls}}`): frequency horizontal, time vertical, signal strength as brightness (G8C14); **vertical lines flanking a signal indicate overmodulation** (G8C13).
- The digital voice trio: **DMR, D-STAR, and System Fusion** (G8C16).
- Vocabulary: Baudot code, mark and space, Varicode, WSPR, ARQ, NAK, FEC, packet header, mesh network, waterfall, digital voice.

## 5. Worked examples (at least one `> **Worked example:**` blockquote; the first two are the chapter's signature math)

- Carson's rule (G8B06): "An FM transmitter with 5 kHz peak deviation and 3 kHz maximum audio: BW ≈ 2 × (5 + 3) = **16 kHz**."
- Deviation through a multiplier chain (G8B07): "A 146.52 MHz FM transmitter built on a 12.21 MHz oscillator: the multiplication factor is 146.52 ÷ 12.21 = 12, and deviation multiplies by the same factor — so for 5 kHz output deviation the oscillator needs only 5,000 ÷ 12 = **416.7 Hz** of deviation."
- Image arithmetic (G8B02, illustrative): "A superhet with a 455 kHz IF: the image response sits twice the IF away — 910 kHz from the desired signal. Twice the IF, not twice the signal frequency."
- IMD order (G8B13): "The order of an intermodulation product is the sum of its mixing coefficients: 2F1 − F2 → 2 + 1 = 3 (odd — it lands close in), while 3F1 − F2 → 4 and 5F1 − 3F2 → 8 (both even)."
