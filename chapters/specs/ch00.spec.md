## 2. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

### Beat 1 — What the General license opens
- The upgrade in one sentence: some operating privileges on **all** amateur bands and all modes — the door to worldwide HF communication (ARRL's own line, pinned in canon §2.4). Contrast with the Technician's everyday VHF/UHF world from Book 2.
- The honest map: General adds transmitting segments on every MF/HF band (2200 m through 10 m) and shares everything above 30 MHz with Technician, Advanced, and Amateur Extra — but the lowest slices of 80/75, 40, 20, and 15 m remain Extra (and partly Advanced) territory (canon §2.4; full drill is ch01's job).
- Why it's worth it: HF phone, more digital spectrum, more power (1.5 kW PEP ceiling vs the Technician's 200 W HF segments), contests and DX at full strength.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch00-what-general-opens}}`.
- Vocabulary: license class, privileges, HF, MF, emission mode.

### Beat 2 — The upgrade exam (Element 3)
- One exam, not two: you pass **Element 3 only** — Element 2 is already credited from the Technician license you hold (§97.501(b) with §97.505(a)).
- The exam's shape: 35 questions, one drawn from each of the pool's 35 groups, out of the public 423-question 2023–2027 General pool (10 subelements G1–G9 and G0); 26 correct to pass (§97.503(b); canon §1.3).
- The pool is public and fixed for exams 2023-07-01 → **2027-06-30** — no surprise questions; every exam question is verbatim in Appendix A of this book. Say the expiry plainly and early: this book tracks the 2023–2027 pool (six errata incorporated; nine withdrawn questions simply don't exist — never look for them).
- Vocabulary: Element 3, question pool, subelement, group, NCVEC, errata.

### Beat 3 — Exam day, for upgraders
- You already have an FRN from your Technician grant — no new CORES step. (If a reader somehow lacks one: registering in CORES "carries no fee and no exam requirement" — never print "free of charge," canon §7.12.)
- Find a session: ARRL's session finder and hamstudy.org/sessions; exams are run by teams of at least 3 VEs under a VEC. **Wording law (canon §7.12):** remote video-supervised exams exist but availability depends entirely on the individual VE team — never promise remote testing.
- Fees: the ARRL VEC session fee is $15.00 ($5.00 under 18 — calendar-2026 figures; Laurel VEC charges nothing). **The upgrade exemption:** the FCC's $35 application fee does not apply to operator-class upgrades — no payment step, no 10-day payment window (contrast with Book 2's new-license flow).
- What to bring: one legal photo ID (or two non-photo IDs), your FRN, the session fee; the NCVEC Quick-Form 605 (2022 edition) is completed at the session — email and FRN mandatory.
- Vocabulary: FRN, CORES, VEC, VE, exam session, Form 605, CSCE.

### Beat 4 — After you pass: operate immediately, with /AG
- The upgrade-immediacy rule (§97.9(b)): once the Form 605 requesting the higher class is properly submitted to the administering VEs **and** you hold the CSCE showing the pass, you may exercise General privileges immediately — before the VEC files anything and before ULS changes — until final disposition of the application or 365 days, whichever comes first.
- **Wording law (canon §7.2, binding):** never write "transmit as soon as you pass" without both conditions (Form 605 properly submitted + CSCE in hand) **and** the /AG identification requirement in the same breath.
- The /AG indicator (§97.119(f)(2), (c)): while using the new privileges, append AG to your call sign — say "temporary AG" on phone ("This is KX9ABC temporary AG"), sign KX9ABC/AG on CW or digital; drop it once ULS shows General. (One VEC's practice, per canon §2.4.)
- Contrast with Book 2's new licensees: a first-time licensee has **no** authority until the grant appears in ULS — immediacy is for existing licensees only. You keep your call sign when you upgrade. Grant timing: "your ULS record typically updates within days" — never print a day count as a promise (canon §7.12).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch00-upgrade-journey}}`.
- Vocabulary: CSCE, ULS, /AG indicator, upgrade authority, final disposition.

### Beat 5 — How this book works (and what's next)
- The map: one pool subelement per chapter, G1→chapter 1 … G0→chapter 10; every chapter ends with an Exam Focus of verbatim pool questions; Appendix A carries all 423 verbatim with one-line whys; the repo's `make_exam.py` builds realistic 35-question practice exams.
- Notation, same as Book 2: prose uses V and × (V = I × R); the General pool states electrical quantities in words, so verbatim quotes never clash. Unit case is load-bearing (kHz, MHz, mA, pF).
- The book assumes your Technician knowledge — Ohm's law, repeater basics, Part 97 fundamentals — and builds from there. The math is real now (reactance, resonance, dB, SWR, PEP), taught step by step.
- What's next after General: Amateur Extra — Element 4 (50 questions, 37 to pass); the current Extra pool runs 2024-07-01 → 2028-06-30; your Element 3 CSCE counts toward Extra for 365 days.
- Vocabulary: (none new — orientation).
